Arranging transportation for an elderly or mobility-impaired family member to medical appointments across the Portland metropolitan area—such as OHSU, Kaiser Sunnyside, Providence Portland, or local dialysis clinics—can be overwhelming. Families are often unaware that they may be entitled to 100% free rides, or they get blindsided by massive out-of-pocket bills from private medical transport companies.

In Oregon, Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT) is divided into two distinct worlds: rides fully covered under the Oregon Health Plan (OHP) via regional Coordinated Care Organization (CCO) brokerages, and private-pay accessible transportation.

Before booking a medical ride in Multnomah, Washington, or Clackamas County, use this checklist to verify free coverage eligibility before paying out of pocket.

## 1. Always check Oregon Health Plan (OHP) Ride to Care coverage first

If the patient is enrolled in the Oregon Health Plan (Medicaid) through a local Coordinated Care Organization (such as **Health Share of Oregon** or **Trillium Community Health Plan**), transportation to and from covered medical, dental, and behavioral health appointments is a **free mandatory benefit**.

- **Who provides it:** In the Portland tri-county area, Health Share of Oregon contracts through **Ride to Care**.
- **Advance notice:** Rides must typically be scheduled at least **two business days in advance** (urgent same-day hospital discharges are accommodated through case managers).
- **Cost:** **$0.** Never pay a private driver or service for a ride that should be scheduled through your CCO’s NEMT brokerage.
- **Ask your clinic:** *"Can your clinic social worker or front desk verify if this appointment is eligible for scheduling through Ride to Care?"*

## 2. Understand the levels of service: Curb vs. Door vs. Bedside assist

Not all medical transport offers the same level of hands-on physical assistance.

- **Curb-to-Curb:** The driver pulls up to the curb and assists the passenger into the vehicle, but will not enter the home or building. (Common for TriMet LIFT paratransit).
- **Door-to-Door:** The driver meets the passenger at the exterior door of the residence and escorts them to the exterior door of the medical clinic.
- **Door-Through-Door (Bedside / Room-to-Room):** Essential for frail patients who cannot navigate stairs or transfer safely from bed to wheelchair. The driver enters the home, assists with transfers, navigates porch steps, and stays with the patient until checked in at the clinic desk.
- **Ask:** *"What level of physical assistance is included in your rate, and does your driver enter the home to assist with wheelchair transfers?"*

## 3. Scrutinize private-pay fee structures

If the patient is not on OHP or is traveling to a non-medical destination (such as a family wedding, funeral, or community center), you must hire a private provider.

- **Base Load Fee:** A flat fee charged just to arrive and load the passenger (typically $45 to $95 for standard wheelchair, $150+ for stretcher/gurney).
- **Mileage Rate:** Billed per one-way mile (typically $3.50 to $6.00 per mile across the Portland metro).
- **Stair Fees:** If the home has more than 3 exterior steps, private companies often charge an additional $30 to $60 "stair carry fee" requiring two staff members.
- **Ask:** *"What is the complete round-trip out-the-door price, including base load, mileage, stair assist, and after-hours surcharges?"*

## 4. Differentiate "Will-Call" returns from "Dedicated Wait-Time"

Medical appointments—especially chemotherapy, dialysis, and surgical consultations—rarely run on a predictable schedule.

- **Will-Call Return:** The driver drops the patient off and leaves. When the appointment finishes, the nurse calls dispatch, and the company sends the next available van (which can take 30 to 90 minutes of waiting in a clinic lobby).
- **Dedicated Wait-and-Return:** The driver remains parked outside the clinic for the entire duration of the appointment and is ready to load the patient immediately upon exit. This typically incurs a $40 to $70 per hour wait fee, but prevents exhausting delays for frail seniors.

## 5. Verify vehicle safety inspections and driver credentials

- Confirm that the vehicle is equipped with crash-tested 4-point wheelchair floor tie-downs and shoulder occupant restraint belts (Q'Straint or Sure-Lok systems).
- Confirm that drivers are certified in First Aid, Adult CPR, and Passenger Assistance Safety and Sensitivity (PASS).
