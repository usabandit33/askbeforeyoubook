Homeowners with dedicated irrigation systems, swimming pools, boilers, or mixed-use properties across Metro Atlanta receive an annual notice from the water authority requiring mandatory backflow testing. Whether your service is administered by the City of Atlanta Department of Watershed Management (DWM), Fulton County Public Works, DeKalb County Watershed, or Cobb County Water System, ignoring this annual test can lead to steep municipal fines and eventual termination of your water service.

A backflow preventer—typically a Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) device or Double Check Valve Assembly (DCVA)—ensures contaminated fertilizer, pesticides, or stagnant water from lawn sprinkler lines cannot siphon backwards into the public drinking water supply during a water main break.

Before booking a backflow technician in Atlanta, use this checklist to guarantee your test is properly completed and legally filed with the correct municipal database.

## 1. Confirm that the service includes electronic filing with the water authority

Testing the assembly is only half the job; the critical administrative step is submitting the certified results to the appropriate water utility before the compliance deadline.

- **Ask:** *"Does your fee include the electronic submission and any third-party portal processing fees (such as BSI Online, VEPO Cross-Connection, or the City of Atlanta DWM Portal)?"*
- **Why it matters:** Some low-cost testers perform the 15-minute gauge test, hand you a handwritten paper carbon copy, and walk away. If you fail to upload the paperwork correctly to your county or city portal, the municipality will still consider you delinquent and dispatch a shutoff notice. A full-service tester enters the test data directly into the official portal on your behalf.

## 2. Verify state and municipal tester certifications

Municipalities across Georgia reject test reports from uncertified contractors.

- **Ask:** *"Is the technician certified through the Georgia Association of Water Professionals (GAWP) or the American Society of Sanitary Engineering (ASSE 5110), and is their calibrated differential pressure gauge certificate up to date?"*
- **Annual gauge calibration:** Test gauges must be recalibrated annually to maintain state certification. If a technician uses an expired gauge, the water authority’s database will reject the submission, requiring a re-test.

## 3. Insist on a rebuild kit before agreeing to a full assembly replacement

Backflow assemblies are heavy bronze devices. If an internal check valve or relief valve fails the pressure differential test due to grit or mineral buildup, replacement is rarely the only option.

- **Ask:** *"If the device fails the initial test, can it be disassembled and cleaned, or repaired with a factory rubber rebuild kit, before quoting a full replacement?"*
- **Cost difference:** A manufacturer rebuild kit (new rubber discs, springs, and O-rings for Watts, Wilkins, or Febco units) usually costs $60 to $150 in parts plus an hour of labor. In contrast, cutting out and soldering a brand new 1-inch RPZ valve can cost $800 to $1,500. Unless the bronze casting has frozen and cracked over winter, a rebuild almost always restores passing specs.

## 4. Protect the assembly from Atlanta winter freezes

Because irrigation backflow assemblies sit above ground outside the home, uninsulated units are vulnerable to freezing during Atlanta cold snaps (sub-25°F temperatures).
- Confirm that the technician tests all four test cocks and leaves the shutoff valves in the proper winterized position if the irrigation system is decommissioned for the season.
- Always use an insulated freeze pouch or insulated hot box over exterior valves.

## 5. Get confirmation and test tracking numbers in writing

- Request an electronic PDF copy of the completed Georgia Test Report signed by the certified tester.
- Request the tracking/submission confirmation number from the municipal portal (e.g., BSI Online confirmation number) to prove your property is compliant in the event of an automated water utility billing audit.
