The warm, humid climate and dense tree canopy across Charlotte and Mecklenburg County—from Dilworth and Myers Park to South End and Ballantyne—create ideal conditions for mold, mildew, and black algae (*Gloeocapsa magma*) to spread across home exteriors. In North Carolina, black roof streaks, green siding growth, and blackened driveways are universal homeowner battles.

However, hiring an untrained operator with a high-pressure commercial pressure washer can destroy your home’s exterior in minutes. Blasting 3,500 PSI water at vinyl siding forces gallons of water behind the panels into your plywood wall sheathing, while pressure-washing an asphalt shingle roof strips away protective ceramic granules and instantly voids manufacturer warranties.

Before booking an exterior cleaning contractor in Charlotte, use this checklist to ensure they use low-pressure soft washing for siding and roofs, reserving high pressure strictly for hard flat concrete.

## 1. Require dedicated low-pressure "soft washing" for siding, stucco, and roofs

Soft washing relies on specialized cleaning chemistry—not brute water force—to kill organic growth at the root.

- **Ask:** *"Do you use a low-pressure soft wash system (under 300 PSI) with dedicated chemical pumps for my roof and siding, or do you use a standard pressure washer wand?"*
- **The difference:** 
  - **Soft Washing (< 100–300 PSI):** Operates at garden hose pressure. A gentle stream applies an eco-friendly surfactant and diluted sodium hypochlorite solution that chemically breaks down the algae and lichen cell walls. The dead growth is then gently rinsed away without touching the delicate siding surface.
  - **Pressure Blasting (2,500–4,000 PSI):** Uses extreme mechanical force. On vinyl, it cracks thin trim and dislodges weeping holes. On painted wood or fiber-cement (Hardie board), it chips paint and drives water into seams. On roofs, the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association (ARMA) explicitly warns that pressure washing destroys shingles.

## 2. Verify landscape, window, and electrical outlet protection

The cleaning solutions used to dissolve algae can scorch delicate shrubs and short out exterior electronics if applied carelessly.

- **Ask:** *"What protocols do you follow to protect my boxwoods, hydrangeas, exterior outlets, and video doorbells?"*
- **Standard precautions:**
  - **Pre-wetting & continuous rinsing:** Landscaping must be saturated with fresh clean water before, during, and after chemical application. Diluting any overspray prevents leaf burn.
  - **Taping electrical fixtures:** Exterior GFCI outlets, keyless entry pads, and Ring doorbells must be taped with water-resistant painters' tape to prevent electrical shorts.
  - **Chemical neutralizers:** Responsible contractors use a plant wash neutralizer (such as sodium thiosulfate) on sensitive ornamental garden beds after cleaning.

## 3. High pressure should be reserved strictly for flat concrete with a surface cleaner

High pressure is appropriate for driveways, patios, and public sidewalks, but only when applied evenly.

- **Ask:** *"Do you clean concrete driveways with a dual-nozzle rotary surface cleaner, or do you swing a handheld wand back and forth?"*
- **Why it matters:** Cleaning a large driveway with a handheld pressure wand leaves unsightly zig-zag wand marks ("zebra striping") etched into the concrete cream. A professional rotary surface cleaner hovers on a water cushion, spinning two balanced spray tips to deliver uniform, streak-free deep cleaning.

## 4. Check for wastewater runoff compliance

In Charlotte, the City of Charlotte and Mecklenburg County Storm Water Services enforce strict environmental ordinances prohibiting commercial chemical wash water from draining directly into municipal storm sewers (which discharge untreated into local creeks like Sugar Creek and Lake Norman).
- Confirm that the contractor directs wash water into grass or landscaped absorption areas, or uses containment booms.

## 5. Verify commercial liability insurance

Never hire an uninsured handyman with a pressure washer bought at a big-box store.
- If high pressure accidentally shatters a double-pane low-E window or forces water into an electrical breaker box, you need proof of a minimum $1,000,000 commercial general liability policy.
- Request a certificate of insurance (COI) sent directly from their insurance broker before work begins.
