Tankless on-demand water heaters are increasingly popular across the Greater Houston area, from the Heights to Katy and Sugar Land. However, because Houston municipal water contains significant dissolved calcium and magnesium (moderate to hard water sourced from Lake Houston, Trinity River, and Gulf Coast aquifers), mineral scale rapidly builds up on the narrow copper heating fins inside the heat exchanger.

When mineral scale thickens, heat transfer drops, energy bills climb, and the unit starts throwing error codes (such as Rinnai code 11/12 or Navien E003/E016) before shutting down mid-shower. Homeowners are often told by commission-driven technicians that the entire $3,000+ unit is ruined, when a thorough 45-to-60 minute descaling flush would restore full function.

Use this checklist before scheduling a tankless repair or maintenance visit in Houston.

## 1. Confirm whether service isolation valves are already installed

A tankless water heater cannot be flushed without isolation service valves (a dedicated set of hot and cold valves equipped with hose bibb purge ports).

- **Ask:** *"Does my current installation already have isolation valves, or will your quote include adding them?"*
- **Why it matters:** If your builder or previous installer skipped isolation valves to save $60, a technician cannot connect a descaling pump without cutting into your pipes. Knowing this ahead of time prevents being surprised by an unexpected $250 to $400 charge on service day.

## 2. Verify the descaling solution and pump duration

Skipping or rushing the flush cycle leaves calcified scale baked onto the heat exchanger walls.

- **Ask:** *"What descaling agent do you use (food-grade white vinegar or non-toxic virgin sulfamic acid cleaner), and how long will the recirculating pump run?"*
- **Standard practice:** A standard preventative flush requires circulating 3 to 4 gallons of solution continuously for a minimum of 45 to 60 minutes. Severely scaled units may require 90 minutes with fresh solution until the discharge runs clear.

## 3. Ensure the cold water inlet filter and burner are serviced

A complete tankless service includes more than circulating acid through the water jacket.

- **Ask:** *"Will your service include removing and cleaning the in-line cold water sediment filter screen, inspecting the exhaust vent termination, and checking flame sensors?"*
- **Houston humidity & storm debris:** In coastal Texas climates, dirt daubers and outdoor insects frequently nest inside tankless direct-vent exterior flues, choking off combustion air. A proper maintenance visit checks the intake screen and fan blower.

## 4. Don’t accept a full replacement quote without a flush test first

If a Houston plumber tells you your 4-to-7-year-old tankless unit has a *"cracked heat exchanger"* solely because hot water turns lukewarm after two minutes, be skeptical.

- **Ask:** *"Has the unit been descaled and the thermistor sensor tested before recommending a complete unit replacement?"*
- Scale buildup acts as thermal insulation. The burner fires at full blast, the internal safety sensor detects extreme heat inside the combustion chamber, and the computer cuts the gas to prevent melting—producing the classic "cold water sandwich." Descaling often completely resolves the issue.

## 5. Check manufacturer warranty documentation

Major manufacturers (Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem) explicitly require routine descaling every 12 to 24 months in areas with hard water like Houston.
- Request an itemized invoice stating the date of service, flushing agent used, and verification of operational water pressure.
- Keep this receipt in your home maintenance records to protect your 12-to-15-year factory heat exchanger warranty against claims of neglected maintenance.
