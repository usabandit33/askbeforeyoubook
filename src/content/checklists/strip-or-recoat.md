Commercial property managers, medical clinic operators, retail business owners, and school facilities directors across Greater Phoenix—from downtown high-rises to industrial parks in Tempe and Chandler—face an ongoing battle against desert dust. Fine caliche dust and sand tracked indoors act like 60-grit sandpaper, abrading the protective clear finish on Vinyl Composition Tile (VCT) and hard surface floors.

When a commercial floor loses its wet-look gloss and begins to look dull and scuffed, facility managers often assume they must pay for an expensive, disruptive full strip-and-wax. In many cases, an economical **scrub-and-recoat** achieves an identical high-gloss shine in half the time and at a fraction of the cost.

Use this commercial floor care checklist to choose the right service level before booking a janitorial contractor in Phoenix.

## 1. Differentiate a scrub-and-recoat from a full strip-and-wax

Understanding the technical process prevents paying for intensive chemical stripping when routine maintenance is all that is needed:

- **Scrub-and-Recoat (Top Scrub):** The technician uses an auto-scrubber or low-speed (175 RPM) floor machine equipped with a green or blue scrubbing pad and an intensive neutral cleaner. It removes only the top 1 to 2 dirty, scratched coats of finish without stripping down to the bare tile. Once rinsed and dried, 1 to 2 fresh coats of high-solids wax are applied. 
  - *Best for:* Routine quarterly or semi-annual maintenance where the underlying finish is intact and not discolored.
- **Full Strip-and-Wax (Deep Restoration):** Aggressive chemical stripper (high pH emulsifier) is applied and agitated with black stripping pads to dissolve 100% of the old wax layers down to raw bare tile. The liquefied sludge is extracted with a commercial wet vacuum, rinsed twice with clean water, neutralized, and rebuilt with 1 coat of sealer and 4 to 5 coats of high-solids floor finish.
  - *Best for:* Floors that have yellowed, are peeling along baseboards, or have heavy ground-in black scuffs that top scrubbing cannot remove.

## 2. Require high-solids commercial polymer finish (20% to 25% solids)

Cheap commercial janitorial services often save money by applying low-grade, watered-down floor wax.

- **Ask:** *"What brand of floor finish do you use, and what is its solid content percentage?"*
- **The benchmark:** High-traffic commercial facilities in Arizona require a commercial acrylic copolymer finish containing **20% to 25% non-volatile solids** (such as Buckeye Castleguard, Betco, or Spartan). Cheap 15% solids finish looks shiny for two weeks, but rapidly wears off in front of entrance doors.

## 3. Verify neutralization rinsing after chemical stripping

The most common reason newly applied floor wax bubbles, peels, or turns cloudy within 30 days is improper neutralization.

- **Ask:** *"What is your rinsing protocol after stripping, and do you test the floor's pH before laying down the first coat of finish?"*
- **Why it matters:** Floor strippers are highly alkaline (pH 12–13). If chemical residue remains on the bare VCT when fresh wax (pH 8–9) is poured, the stripper attacks the new wax from underneath, turning it into a brittle white powder that flakes off under foot traffic. A professional crew rinses with clean water and a mild acidic/neutral neutralizing agent, verifying a neutral pH of 7 before coating.

## 4. Insist on baseboard and corner detailing

Slapping mop heads against walls splatters sticky wax and dirty stripper onto vinyl cove baseboards.
- **Ask:** *"Do your crews scrape corners and remove wax buildup from vinyl baseboards using doodlebug scrub pads?"*
- Walk your facility: If baseboards are covered in yellowed, dried wax ridges, the previous crew was cutting corners. A true deep strip includes hand-scraping all edges, corners, and baseboards.

## 5. High-speed burnishing for long-term durability

- After the final coat of finish has cured (typically 24 to 48 hours), high-speed propane or battery burnishing (1,500 to 2,500 RPM) thermal-bonds the acrylic polymers, creating an ultra-hard, scratch-resistant "mirror gloss" that repels desert sand.
