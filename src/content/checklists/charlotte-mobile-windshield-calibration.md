Modern vehicles use windshield-mounted cameras for lane keep, automatic emergency braking, and other ADAS features. After glass replacement those cameras usually need recalibration. Use these questions before you book a mobile or shop service in the Charlotte area.

## Questions to ask before you book

* **Does my vehicle need static, dynamic, or both calibrations?** Ask the technician to confirm from the OEM procedure whether your model needs a static target-board setup, a dynamic road test, or both (Auto Glass Safety Council guidance; manufacturer service information).
* **Will you perform the calibration on site, and do you bring the diagnostic equipment?** Mobile service should include the scan tool and targets needed so you are not left driving uncalibrated to a second location.
* **Will I receive a signed calibration report or certificate?** Ask for a diagnostic printout showing the systems that were calibrated and that they meet OEM tolerances. Keep it with your repair records.
* **Is ADAS recalibration included in the glass quote, or is it separate?** Get the full price and scope in writing, including any re-calibration if warning lights remain.
* **Does my insurance cover the calibration?** Review your comprehensive glass coverage; many policies treat required ADAS recalibration as part of the glass claim. Confirm deductible and preferred providers.
* **What happens if a system still shows a warning light?** Ask about post-calibration road testing and the process for returning if a feature does not function.
* **Are your technicians trained to AGSC or equivalent ADAS standards?** Ask about certification and the specific tools used for your vehicle make.

## Why calibration matters after a Charlotte windshield replacement

A rock chip or crack on I-77, I-85, or I-485 often means a full windshield replacement. When the glass comes out, the forward-facing camera (and sometimes related sensors) loses its precise alignment. Leaving the system uncalibrated can cause distance miscalculations for automatic emergency braking, lane-departure warnings, or adaptive cruise. Traditional shops sometimes treat the glass install as complete and send the vehicle to a dealership for calibration days later. Mobile calibration brings the diagnostic hardware to your driveway or workplace so the car leaves the job fully checked. Before you drive any distance after glass work, confirm whether your ADAS systems require calibration and whether that work is finished and documented.
