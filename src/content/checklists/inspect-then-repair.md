Commercial property managers, multi-family building owners, and business operators across Dallas—from downtown commercial towers to warehouses along the I-35 corridor—are required by city ordinance and insurance underwriters to perform annual fire alarm and fire sprinkler inspections. In Texas, these inspections are governed by the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) State Fire Marshal's Office (SFMO) and enforced locally by Dallas Fire-Rescue (DFR).

A widespread industry practice among aggressive fire protection contractors is using the annual inspection as a loss-leader. They offer a cut-rate inspection fee, then generate an alarming list of "mandatory deficiencies," quoting inflated parts and emergency labor rates while holding the building’s compliance certificate hostage.

Before signing a fire protection service contract in Dallas, use this checklist to separate routine NFPA testing from deficiency repair authorizations.

## 1. Always separate the annual inspection contract from deficiency repair work

Never sign a blanket agreement that gives an inspection company pre-authorization to perform repairs while on site.

- **Ask:** *"Is your quote strictly for the annual testing and reporting under NFPA 72 (fire alarm) and NFPA 25 (fire sprinkler), with all repair deficiencies quoted separately in writing?"*
- **Why it matters:** An inspection is an objective audit. If the company is incentivized to find billable repairs, minor issues (such as an aging backup battery or an illuminated exit sign with a weak bulb) are frequently billed at 300% to 500% markup. Requiring a separate, itemized deficiency quote allows you to review the findings, verify code requirements, and solicit competitive bids for major repairs.

## 2. Understand Texas State Fire Marshal tagging rules (Blue, Yellow, and Red tags)

In Texas, licensed fire inspectors are legally required to attach color-coded physical tags to your fire alarm panel and sprinkler riser:

- **Blue Tag (Compliant):** The system was tested in accordance with NFPA standards and has zero deficiencies. It is good for one full year.
- **Yellow Tag (Operational with Non-Critical Deficiencies):** The system functions as intended, but has minor non-compliance issues (e.g., missing signs, past-due 5-year internal pipe inspection, or painted sprinkler heads). The system remains in service, and the owner typically has 30 to 60 days to complete repairs without facing immediate municipal fines.
- **Red Tag (System Impaired):** A critical failure exists (e.g., main water valve closed, panel ground fault preventing alarm horns from sounding, or frozen dry pipe riser). Under Texas law, a Red Tag must be reported directly to the local Dallas Fire Marshal within 24 to 48 hours, which may require posting an around-the-clock "Fire Watch" until resolved.

## 3. Demand exact NFPA code citations for every cited deficiency

If an inspector flags an item as "mandatory," require them to prove it in the written report.

- **Ask:** *"What is the specific NFPA chapter and section number requiring this repair, and what were the exact test readings (such as battery voltage, smoke detector sensitivity percentage, or waterflow switch trip seconds)?"*
- Vague notes like *"batteries weak"* or *"heads dirty"* are unacceptable. A professional Texas inspection report includes precise device address numbers, decibel levels, and calibrated gauge pressure readings.

## 4. Verify Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) licensing

In Texas, fire protection companies and technicians must hold specialized state licenses issued by the State Fire Marshal's Office.
- **Fire Alarm:** The company must hold an Alarm Certificate of Registration (ACR), and technicians must hold an individual Fire Alarm License (FAL).
- **Fire Sprinkler:** The company must hold a Sprinkler Certificate of Registration (SCR), and technicians must hold a Responsible Managing Employee (RME) license.
- Always verify active license status on the official [TDI State Fire Marshal Licensing Search](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/fire/) before work begins.

## 5. Check Dallas Fire-Rescue (DFR) portal reporting compliance

In Dallas, fire inspection companies must submit completed annual test reports electronically into the city’s approved third-party compliance database (such as Compliance Engine / Brycer).
- Confirm that the contractor uploads the completed report directly to the city portal and provides you with the digital submission confirmation receipt for your property records.
