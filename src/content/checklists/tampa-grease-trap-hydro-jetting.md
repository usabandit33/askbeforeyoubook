## Questions to ask before you book

* **Do you hydro-jet the outlet lines and interceptor walls, or only pump the trap?** Ask whether the service includes high-pressure water jetting to strip hardened FOG from internal drain walls, not just liquid removal.
* **How often should we schedule jetting in addition to regular pumping?** Many operators combine pumping with jetting on a set interval (for example every six months); ask for a recommendation based on your kitchen volume and local FOG rules.
* **Will you inspect outlet sanitary tees and baffles?** Confirm they check for calcified grease buildup that can cause sudden kitchen backups.
* **Do you use or recommend chemical degreasers or additives?** Under City of Tampa Grease Management rules, chemicals, enzymes, emulsifiers, or other grease cutters used in interceptors or traps generally need prior approval from the GMP inspector. Confirm current guidance before agreeing.
* **Are you equipped and permitted for commercial grease waste lines?** Ask about licensing, disposal, and whether they handle restaurant interceptors specifically under Tampa and Hillsborough County FOG programs.
* **What documentation will I receive?** Keep dated service logs, manifests, and pumping/jetting records for health and environmental inspections (retain as required by your sewer utility or FOG program).
* **What is the price and emergency availability?** Get the scope in writing, including after-hours or emergency rates if drains are already slow or backing up.

## Preventing kitchen floods with commercial hydro-jetting in Tampa

Tampa’s booming dining and entertainment districts put intense pressure on commercial kitchen drainage. While standard grease interceptor pumping removes floating waste and bottom sludge, it often leaves behind a stubborn layer of grease coating the interior walls of outlet pipes. Over time, this buildup narrows the pipe diameter until a complete blockage occurs during a peak service period.

Standard plumbing companies often avoid greasy commercial waste lines due to the messy nature of the work. When slow drains threaten to shut down a kitchen or trigger health-inspector warnings, restaurant managers need immediate, heavy-duty intervention rather than another routine pump-out.

Specialized commercial hydro-jetting services address this directly. By using high-pressure water streams to scour grease down to bare pipe walls, these specialists restore full drainage capacity, protect kitchens from catastrophic backups, and help operators stay compliant with municipal environmental codes.

Local context that matters: City of Tampa Code Chapter 26, Article VII (Grease Management) requires food service facilities to maintain interceptors and traps, with pumping triggers tied to settled solids and the common 25% rule (when captured grease and solids displace more than about 25% of the device volume, or when solids rise to the outlet invert). Hillsborough County industrial pretreatment rules also emphasize minimum service intervals (commonly every 90 days for professionally serviced devices) and the 25% / depth limits. Pair scheduled pumping with periodic jetting of outlet lines, keep clear service records, and confirm current Tampa Water / Hillsborough County FOG expectations before you sign a contract.
