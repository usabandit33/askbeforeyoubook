When a contractor includes a permit line on a quote, the amount is not always the same as the fee the city publishes. The difference can come from how the contractor estimates the fee, whether the line includes the contractor's time to pull the permit, or other costs the city does not charge.

For Phoenix homeowners, the useful question is what the city actually charges for the specific work and whether the contractor's permit line matches that figure or adds something else.

## 1. Find the city's published fee for the work

Phoenix publishes fee schedules for building, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, and other permits. The amount depends on the type of work, the valuation, and sometimes the square footage or other factors.

Before comparing quotes, look up the relevant fee schedule on the City of Phoenix website. Note the base fee and any additional charges that apply to your project. A contractor who quotes a permit fee far above the published amount should be able to explain the difference.

## 2. Ask what the contractor's permit line includes

A permit line on a contractor's estimate may cover only the city fee, or it may include the contractor's cost to prepare the application, submit plans, and respond to questions from the city.

Ask the contractor:

- Is the permit amount the exact city fee, or does it include your time?
- Who is responsible for submitting the application and obtaining the permit?
- What happens if the city requires additional information or a revision?
- Will you provide a copy of the approved permit before work begins?

Get the answers in writing. A clear statement that the line matches the published city fee, or a breakdown that separates the city fee from the contractor's handling charge, makes the quote easier to compare.

## 3. Confirm who pulls the permit and who is listed

The permit should list the contractor performing the work. Ask whether the company that quotes the job will be the permit holder or whether a subcontractor will be listed.

Also confirm that the contractor will not begin work until the permit is issued. Starting without a required permit can create problems with inspections, insurance, and future sale of the home.

## 4. Compare the total price, not just the permit line

Two quotes may show different permit amounts for the same work. The difference is not necessarily a red flag if one contractor includes handling time and the other does not. Compare the total price for the same scope and ask each contractor to explain its permit figure.

If the permit line is unusually high, request the city's published fee for comparison. If it is unusually low, ask whether the contractor expects the homeowner to pull the permit or whether additional fees will appear later.

## Before you book

Use the matching Phoenix permit checklist to compare the contractor's permit line with the city's published fee. Confirm who will pull the permit and whether the amount is the city fee only or includes the contractor's handling time.
