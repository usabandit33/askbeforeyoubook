Stumps left after tree work get in the way of mowing, planting, and outdoor use. Grinding reduces the stump below grade; full root removal or hauling chips is a different scope. Quotes that only list a “per stump” price can hide depth, access, and debris handling. The useful questions are how deep the grind goes, what happens to the chips, and whether equipment can reach the stump safely.

## 1. Confirm grind depth and what is left in place

Ask how far below grade the grinder will cut and whether surface roots in the work area are included. A shallow grind may still catch a mower; a deeper grind costs more but leaves a cleaner area for sod or planting.

Clarify whether the quote is for one stump or multiple, and how diameter is measured.

## 2. Decide on chip disposal and site cleanup

Grinding produces a large pile of chips and soil. Ask:

- Are chips left on site, spread, or hauled away?
- Is haul-away included in the price or extra?
- Will the hole be backfilled or left open?

Get disposal and cleanup in writing so you are not comparing a grind-only price to a grind-and-haul job.

## 3. Check access, utilities, and extras

Confirm that the machine can reach the stump without damaging lawns, fences, or hardscape. Ask whether underground utilities will be marked and whether steep slopes, tight gates, or rear-yard access change the price.
