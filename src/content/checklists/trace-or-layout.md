Commercial property owners, HOA board members, and business managers across the Denver metro area—from Lower Downtown (LoDo) and Cherry Creek to Aurora and Lakewood—frequently schedule parking lot line striping after asphalt sealcoating or winter snowplow damage.

However, parking lot painting involves far more than laying down fresh lines. In Denver, a simple striping project can trigger severe civil liability if accessible parking stalls fail to comply with federal Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) standards, or if local fire inspectors issue citations for non-compliant fire lane curbs.

Before signing a striping contract in Denver, use this checklist to separate a simple "re-trace" from a full "new layout," and ensure full municipal code compliance.

## 1. Differentiate a simple "re-stripe" from a "new layout"

Contractor quotes vary dramatically based on whether existing line markers are visible:

- **Re-Stripe (Re-Trace):** The striping machine operator follows existing, visible paint lines on cured asphalt. The crew uses the old lines as a guide, spraying a fresh, crisp coat of traffic paint. This is faster and costs significantly less per linear foot.
- **New Layout:** Required if the asphalt was recently repaved or heavily sealcoated (completely blacking out old markings), or if stall angles (90-degree vs. 60-degree angled parking) are being redesigned to maximize parking capacity. The crew must pull tape measures, calculate stall dimensions, and snap hundreds of chalk lines across the lot before spraying.
- **Ask:** *"Is this quote for an existing line re-trace or a full new chalk layout, and are all stall dimension measurements verified?"*

## 2. Verify strict ADA accessibility compliance

Failure to maintain compliant handicap parking is the #1 trigger for costly ADA accessibility lawsuits in Colorado.

- **Stall & Aisle Dimensions:** Standard accessible stalls must be at least 96 inches (8 feet) wide with an adjacent 60-inch access aisle. At least one in every six accessible stalls must be **Van-Accessible** (either an 8-foot stall with an 8-foot (96-inch) access aisle, or an 11-foot (132-inch) stall with a 5-foot aisle).
- **Slope and Level:** Access aisles must be painted with diagonal hatch lines and must connect directly to an accessible curb ramp leading to the building entrance. The slope of the stall cannot exceed 1:48 (approximately 2.08%) in any direction.
- **Signage:** Under federal rules, ground paint stencils alone are not sufficient. Each accessible space must have a permanent post-mounted sign displaying the International Symbol of Accessibility mounted at least 60 inches above the ground.

## 3. Choose snowplow- and UV-resistant commercial traffic paint

Denver’s intense high-altitude ultraviolet sun and winter snowplow blades rapidly destroy cheap, low-grade latex paint.

- **Ask:** *"What specification of traffic paint do you spray, and do you apply glass retroreflective beads for nighttime visibility?"*
- **Standards:** Commercial lots require fast-dry 100% acrylic waterborne traffic paint meeting federal specification **TT-P-1952F (Type I or Type II)**. 
- For stop bars, crosswalks, and high-wear directional arrows, consider **preformed thermoplastic** or hot-applied thermoplastic, which melts into the asphalt and withstands metal snowplow blades far better than liquid paint.

## 4. Comply with City of Denver Fire Department red curb rules

Commercial centers, multi-family apartment complexes, and office parks must maintain designated emergency vehicle access lanes under the Denver Fire Code.

- **Ask:** *"Does your crew paint fire lane curbs solid traffic red with 4-inch white stencils reading 'NO PARKING - FIRE LANE' spaced every 25 to 30 feet?"*
- A professional contractor verifies that fire hydrant clear zones (minimum 15 feet in each direction) and sprinkler connection loading zones are crisply marked to prevent local fire marshal citations.

## 5. Verify asphalt surface preparation and cure times

Painting over dirt, gravel, or fresh sealcoating results in immediate paint failure.
- Ensure the contractor includes commercial high-velocity air blowing or mechanical sweeping to remove gravel and dust before painting.
- If the lot was just sealcoated, wait a minimum of 24 to 48 hours for the coal-tar or asphalt emulsion sealer to cure completely; spraying paint over fresh sealer causes chemical bleed-through, turning crisp yellow and white lines a muddy brown.
