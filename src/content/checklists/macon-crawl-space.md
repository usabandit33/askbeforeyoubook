Central Georgia humidity, clay soils, and older vented foundations make moisture control under the house a practical concern. A basic ground liner reduces vapor rising from the soil. Full closed encapsulation seals the space more completely and often includes a dehumidifier. The right approach depends on measured moisture, existing damage, and how the space will be used.

## 1. Understand liner versus encapsulation

A vapor liner (typically heavy polyethylene) covers the soil and is a lower-cost first step.

Closed encapsulation usually includes:

- Sealed ground barrier with taped seams
- Coverage on walls and piers
- Closing or conditioning of foundation vents
- A dehumidifier sized for the space
- Optional insulation and drainage improvements

Ask the company which system it is proposing and why. Request photos or a written description of coverage areas.

## 2. Ask about moisture testing and current conditions

Before choosing a system, confirm:

- Has relative humidity or moisture content been measured?
- Are there signs of standing water, mold, or wood decay?
- Is exterior drainage or grading contributing to the problem?
- Will vents stay open, be closed, or be replaced with conditioned air?

A lower price may reflect liner-only work. Confirm the full scope.

## 3. Compare written scope, materials, and warranty

Get the material thickness (mil rating), coverage details, equipment brand, and warranty terms in writing. Ask whether the warranty requires annual maintenance or a service plan.
