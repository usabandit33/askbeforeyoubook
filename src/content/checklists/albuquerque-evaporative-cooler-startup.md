Albuquerque’s dry climate makes evaporative (swamp) coolers a practical cooling choice, but they need seasonal startup work after sitting idle through winter. Use these questions before you book a spring service.

## Questions to ask before you book

* **Do you replace aspen or rigid media pads as part of startup?** Ask whether old, scale-encrusted pads are swapped for new ones and what type they use for your unit.
* **Do you flush and scrub the water reservoir?** Confirm they remove mineral scale, calcium deposits, and dust from the basin before filling for the season.
* **Do you test the water pump and float valve?** Ask that they verify pump operation, electrical draw, and that the float maintains correct level without overflow.
* **Do you oil blower bearings, motor, and check the belt?** Confirm lubrication of bearings and pulleys and inspection or replacement of the drive belt.
* **What else is included in a spring startup?** Ask about water-line inspection, distribution tubes, bleed-off adjustment, and a final run test with airflow check.
* **What is the price and what is written in the scope?** Get a clear list of included parts (pads, float, belt) versus extras, and whether a follow-up visit is covered if something fails early.
* **Are you insured for roof or elevated work?** Many units are rooftop-mounted; ask about liability coverage and fall protection.

## Why spring startup matters in Albuquerque

Evaporative coolers sit unused through cold months. Pads harden with mineral scale from hard water, pumps seize, dust settles in the housing, and belts dry out. When the first hot days arrive, homeowners often discover weak airflow, musty odors, overflowing pans, or no cooling at all. Local HVAC companies frequently focus on refrigerated central-air installs and treat basic swamp-cooler startups as low priority. A thorough spring service—new pads where needed, clean reservoir, working pump and float, lubricated bearings, and a documented test—gets the unit ready before peak summer heat. Whether you do part of the work yourself or hire it out, follow the manufacturer’s instructions for your specific cooler model and confirm any local water or drainage rules that apply to your property.
