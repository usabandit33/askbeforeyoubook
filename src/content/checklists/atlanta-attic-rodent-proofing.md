Mice, rats, and squirrels readily enter Atlanta attics through small gaps. Traditional trapping alone often leaves the entry points open. Use these questions before you book a permanent exclusion service.

## Questions to ask before you book

* **Will you perform a full inspection of roofline, soffits, gable vents, and pipe penetrations?** Entry points can be as small as a dime for mice or a quarter for rats. Ask how thoroughly they search.
* **Do you seal with chew-proof materials (hardware cloth / steel mesh)?** Confirm they replace flimsy plastic screens and close gaps with materials rodents cannot chew through.
* **Do you address tree and vegetation access?** Ask whether they recommend or include trimming overhanging limbs (typically 6–8 feet from the roofline) that give squirrels and raccoons an easy path.
* **Is cleanup and sanitization of contaminated insulation included?** Pheromone trails and droppings can attract new animals; ask what is included in the scope.
* **Is this exclusion work, or only trapping?** Clarify that the goal is permanent sealing of entry points, not just catch-and-release or monthly bait stations.
* **Are you insured for attic and roof work?** Ask about liability coverage and whether they work in confined, insulated spaces.
* **What is the written scope and warranty?** Get the list of entry points sealed, materials used, and any follow-up guarantee in writing.

## Why permanent exclusion matters in Atlanta

Atlanta’s climate and tree cover make residential attics attractive to mice, rats, and squirrels. Once inside, animals can damage wiring, insulation, and framing. Many pest-control visits focus on trapping the current animals while leaving structural gaps open, so new animals return. General contractors often decline the tight, dusty crawl-and-seal work required for real exclusion. A specialized attic rodent-proofing service concentrates on finding and sealing every practical entry point with chew-resistant materials, then cleaning and sanitizing as needed. That approach stops the cycle instead of managing it month after month. Always confirm local wildlife and pest regulations and use licensed, insured technicians for attic work.
