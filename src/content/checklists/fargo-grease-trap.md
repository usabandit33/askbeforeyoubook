Commercial kitchens in Fargo rely on grease traps to keep FOG (fats, oils, and grease) out of the sewer. When pumping is delayed, drains slow, odors rise, and health or city requirements can be violated. The decision is not only who can pump the trap, but whether the service includes cleaning, inspection, and a written record of what was removed.

## 1. Separate pumping from a full clean and inspect

Pumping removes liquid and sludge. A more complete service may also clean trap walls, check baffles and the outlet, and note capacity or condition. Ask the company what is included so you are not comparing a simple pump to a documented clean-and-inspect job.

## 2. Require a written manifest or service ticket

Ask for a dated receipt that lists approximate volume removed and where the waste was taken. Documentation protects the business and shows compliance when inspectors or landlords ask.

Confirm the company is equipped and permitted for commercial grease-trap work and proper disposal.

## 3. Match frequency to kitchen volume

Busy restaurants may need more frequent service than a light-use kitchen. Ask for a recommended interval based on your seating, menu, and trap size, and get the per-visit price in writing—including after-hours or emergency rates if drains are already backing up.

Use the matching Fargo grease-trap checklist to confirm pump-versus-clean scope, documentation, and pricing before scheduling, especially if the kitchen is already having drain problems.
