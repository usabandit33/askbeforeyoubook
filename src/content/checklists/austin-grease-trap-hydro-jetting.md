For restaurant and commercial kitchen operators in Austin. Standard grease-trap pumping removes floating FOG and sludge, but hardened grease on pipe walls often needs high-pressure hydro-jetting. Use these questions before you book.

## Questions to ask before you book

* **Do you hydro-jet the outlet lines and interceptor walls, or only pump the trap?** Ask whether the service includes high-pressure water jetting to strip hardened FOG from internal drain walls, not just liquid removal.
* **How often should we schedule jetting in addition to regular pumping?** Many operators combine pumping with jetting on a set interval (for example every six months); ask for a recommendation based on your kitchen volume.
* **Will you inspect outlet sanitary tees and baffles?** Confirm they check for calcified grease buildup that can cause sudden backups.
* **Do you use or recommend chemical degreasers?** Many municipal FOG programs restrict or prohibit harsh chemical drain openers that push grease downstream or damage seals. Confirm against Austin guidance.
* **Are you equipped and permitted for commercial grease waste lines?** Ask about licensing, disposal, and whether they handle restaurant interceptors specifically.
* **What documentation will I receive?** Keep dated service logs of pumping and jetting dates for Austin Public Health and environmental inspections.
* **What is the price and emergency availability?** Get the scope in writing, including after-hours or emergency rates if drains are already slow or backing up.

## Preventing kitchen backups with hydro-jetting in Austin

Austin’s restaurant density puts continuous FOG load on commercial drains. Pumping removes floating grease and settled solids, but a stubborn layer can remain on the walls of outlet pipes. Over time that buildup narrows the line until a blockage appears during peak service. Standard plumbing companies often avoid greasy commercial waste lines. Specialized hydro-jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the pipe interior, restore capacity, and reduce the chance of health-department issues. Pair scheduled pumping with periodic jetting, keep clear service records, and confirm current Austin Water / Public Health FOG expectations before you sign a contract.
