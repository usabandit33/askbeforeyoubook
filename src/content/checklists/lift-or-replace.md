Sunken sidewalks, tilted driveway panels, and sloping patio slabs are common across Central Ohio. Columbus sits on dense glacial till and clay-heavy soils that expand when saturated and contract during dry summer stretches. Combined with dozens of annual winter freeze-thaw cycles and poorly directed downspouts, the gravel base beneath concrete slabs frequently washes out, creating hollow voids that cause heavy slabs to drop.

Homeowners facing a trip hazard citation from the City of Columbus or looking to fix an uneven driveway apron often wonder: Should I lift the concrete with polyurethane foam, use traditional mudjacking, or pay for a full tear-out and repour?

Use this checklist to evaluate contractor quotes and choose the right repair method.

## 1. Compare polyurethane foam lifting vs. traditional mudjacking

Both methods inject material beneath the sunken slab to fill voids and hydraulically raise the concrete back to its original grade, but their materials differ dramatically:

- **Polyurethane Foam Injection (PolyLevel/PolyLift):** High-density expanding foam is injected through tiny 5/8-inch holes (about the size of a dime). The foam expands 15–20 times its volume, cures in 15 minutes, and weighs only 2 to 4 pounds per cubic foot. Because it is completely hydrophobic (waterproof), rain runoff and groundwater will not erode it.
- **Traditional Mudjacking (Slabjacking):** A heavy slurry of pulverized limestone, soil, water, and Portland cement is pumped under the slab through 1.5 to 2-inch holes (the size of a soda can). It requires 24 to 48 hours to cure and adds 100 to 120 pounds per cubic foot of dead weight over already-compromised clay soil.
- **Ask:** *"What material do you inject, what size are the drill holes, and how do you patch them to match weathered concrete?"*

## 2. Know when a slab must be replaced instead of lifted

Lifting cannot fix cracked or crumbling concrete. It only restores elevation to intact slabs.

- **Lift candidate:** The slab is in one or two large solid pieces with a clean break, but has settled 1 to 4 inches below the adjacent panel.
- **Tear-out candidate:** The slab is shattered into spiderweb fractures (alligator cracking), exhibits surface scaling from winter de-icing salts, or has crumbled edges where rebar has corroded. Injecting foam or slurry under fractured concrete will simply tilt individual fragments without creating an even plane.
- **Ask:** *"Will raising this panel cause new stress fractures, or is the slab intact enough to lift cleanly?"*

## 3. Fix the underlying water drainage problem first

Concrete does not sink without a reason. In Columbus, 80% of sunken walkway and garage approach slabs are caused by roof downspouts dumping rainwater directly alongside the foundation or pavement edge.

- **Ask:** *"Did your inspection identify where the sub-base eroded, and does your quote include sealing control joints and cracks?"*
- **Crucial step:** If you lift the slab but leave an open joint or downspout discharging onto the corner, the next spring thaw will wash out the remaining sub-base and drop the slab again. Always install downspout extensions and apply a flexible polyurethane joint sealant.

## 4. Check City of Columbus sidewalk trip hazard standards

The City of Columbus Department of Public Service requires property owners to maintain adjacent public sidewalks. Under local municipal code, a vertical displacement (lip) of 1/2 inch or greater constitutes a code violation and safety hazard.
- If you received a city sidewalk repair notice, confirm that the contractor will raise the slab completely flush with adjacent panels (less than 1/4 inch tolerance) to satisfy municipal inspection.

## 5. Get written warranties against re-settlement

- **Ask:** *"What is your written warranty against re-settlement, and does it cover material failure, labor, and warranty re-pumps?"*
- Standard polyurethane warranties in Ohio range from 2 to 5 years. Beware of contractors who offer "lifetime" verbal promises without written exclusions regarding downspouts and soil erosion.
