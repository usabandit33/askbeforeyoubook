A crawl space can collect moisture, odors, and pests. In Charlotte, humidity and soil conditions make moisture control important. Two common approaches are a basic vapor liner and a more complete closed encapsulation system. The right choice depends on the current moisture level, the condition of the foundation vents, and whether the space will be used for storage or mechanical equipment.

## 1. Understand the difference between a liner and encapsulation

A vapor liner (often polyethylene sheeting) covers the ground to reduce moisture rising from the soil. It is a simpler and usually less expensive step.

Closed encapsulation typically includes:

- A sealed ground liner
- Wall and pier coverage
- Sealing or closing foundation vents
- A dehumidifier or other moisture control
- Insulation on the walls or floor above, depending on the design

Ask the company which approach it is proposing and why. A liner alone may be enough for a dry space. Encapsulation is usually recommended when moisture is already high or when the homeowner wants a more controlled environment.

## 2. Ask about current moisture and ventilation

Before choosing a system, ask whether the company measured humidity or inspected for standing water, mold, or wood rot. Also ask whether foundation vents should stay open, be closed, or be replaced with conditioned-air supply.

Confirm:

- What is the current relative humidity in the crawl space?
- Are there signs of bulk water or poor drainage?
- Will the proposed work include sealing vents or adding mechanical ventilation?

## 3. Compare the written scope and warranty

Get a clear description of materials, coverage areas, and any equipment included. Ask what the warranty covers and whether it requires annual maintenance or a service contract.

A lower price may reflect a liner-only job. Confirm the scope before comparing totals.
