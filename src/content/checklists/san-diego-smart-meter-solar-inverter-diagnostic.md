## Questions to ask before you book

* **Will you compare inverter/monitoring production against the SDG&E smart meter export data?** A gap between the app (Enphase, SolarEdge, Tesla, SMA) and utility export readings can point to CT polarity issues, failed production meters, or under-reporting inverters. Ask how they reconcile the two.
* **Do you diagnose microinverter or string inverter communication failures on site?** Confirm they carry tools for Enphase Envoy/gateway, SolarEdge, and common string inverters—and that they will photograph error codes rather than simply reset and leave.
* **Can you check for NEM 2.0 status and the impact of any inverter swap?** Many San Diego systems remain on NEM 2.0; a like-for-like replacement can preserve the original rate treatment, while certain upgrades may trigger a new interconnection. Ask them to document the current interconnection agreement and warranty remaining.
* **Will you inspect AC and DC disconnects, the solar breaker, and CT placement before opening equipment?** Simple trips or loose CT clamps cause a large share of “dead system” calls. Confirm they start with non-invasive checks.
* **Are you a licensed California electrical contractor for this work?** Inverter and meter-side diagnostics involve energized equipment. Ask for license number and whether they carry the correct diagnostic tools for the brands on your roof.
* **What is included in the diagnostic visit price?** Get trip charge, diagnosis-only vs. repair, parts availability, and any follow-up warranty in writing. Ask whether they leave a written fault report you can use with the manufacturer or utility.
* **Will you leave documentation of codes, photos, and recommended next steps?** Keep records for warranty claims, SDG&E disputes, or future service.

## Why San Diego systems need specialized diagnostics

San Diego’s early solar boom left a large installed base of systems that are now past the original installer warranty window. Homeowners commonly see production that is 20% or more below the same month last year with no new shade, red or orange inverter status lights, individual microinverters that stop reporting, or utility bills that no longer show the expected export credits.

Many solar sales organizations focus on new footprints and multi-family projects and treat residential troubleshooting as low priority. That leaves owners of underperforming arrays waiting while the system sits offline or silently under-produces. Specialized diagnostic technicians who carry brand-specific tools, understand SDG&E net-metering data, and can document whether a problem is the inverter, the production meter/CTs, the gateway, or the utility meter itself close this gap.

Local context that matters: SDG&E smart meters record bi-directional flow for net metering. Comparing the inverter monitoring portal to the utility’s export readings is one of the fastest ways to separate a true production failure from a metering or communication fault. Always use a licensed electrician for work inside the inverter, AC disconnect, or main panel. Do not open equipment yourself.
