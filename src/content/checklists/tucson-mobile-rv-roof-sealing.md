Tucson attracts large snowbird and local RV populations, but extreme desert heat bakes rubber and fiberglass roofs until sealants crack and split. When a monsoon storm hits, leaks can ruin interior cabinetry quickly. Traditional dealerships often require you to drive the unit in and leave it in a service yard for weeks. Use these questions before you book an on-site mobile roof sealing or leak repair visit.

## Questions to ask before you book

* **Will you perform an on-site moisture survey before resealing?** Ask whether they use a moisture meter or infrared tools to map wet decking and soft spots, not just visual caulk lines. A surface reseal over wet core can trap moisture and worsen rot.
* **What roof membrane type do you work on, and which products do you use?** EPDM, TPO, fiberglass, and aluminum roofs need different cleaners, primers, and coatings. Confirm they identify your membrane and use compatible sealants and coating systems—not a one-product-fits-all approach.
* **Do you clean, dry, and prime before applying new sealant or coating?** Heat and UV degrade existing caulk; simply adding more on top of dirty or chalked surfaces often fails early. Ask for the surface-prep steps in writing.
* **Is the quote for emergency leak stop, full seam reseal, or full roof recoating?** Scope and price differ widely. Get a clear description of which seams, penetrations, vents, and edges are included, and whether interior ceiling stains are investigated.
* **Can you work at my storage lot, residential pad, or RV park without towing?** Mobile service is the point for many Tucson owners. Confirm access requirements, height clearances, and any park or HOA rules they need you to arrange.
* **What is the warranty on labor and materials, and what voids it?** Ask how long the seal or coating is warranted, whether walk-on traffic or later equipment installs affect coverage, and what documentation you receive (photos, product data sheets).
* **Are you insured for work on customer property, and do you carry the right tools for roof height and desert conditions?** Confirm liability coverage and that they do not need you to supply ladders or staging for typical Class A/C and travel-trailer heights.

## Why Tucson RV roofs fail—and why mobile service matters

Tucson’s climate is hard on RV roofs. Intense UV and daytime heat dry and crack lap sealants, self-leveling caulk around vents and antennas, and the edges of EPDM and TPO membranes. Monsoon storms then drive wind-blown rain into those openings. Once water reaches the wood or composite decking, soft spots and interior ceiling damage follow quickly—especially on units that sit in storage lots between trips.

Dealership service bays fill up during peak snowbird season. Owners are often told to drop the coach and wait days or weeks while the unit occupies a yard space. Mobile technicians who come to the storage lot or residential pad eliminate that delay for many sealing, recoating, and leak-isolation jobs. They are not a substitute for structural rebuilds that truly need a controlled shop, but for moisture checks, seam work, vent reseals, and many full-roof coating systems they close a real gap in local availability.

Local context that matters: work at commercial storage facilities and RV parks may require gate codes, insurance certificates, or advance notice to management. Confirm access before the appointment. Desert dust and heat also affect product cure times—ask the technician how temperature and surface cleanliness change their process on a typical Tucson summer or monsoon day. Always have a licensed, insured tech for roof work; walking on a soft or wet roof is a fall and puncture hazard.

Before the visit, note every interior stain, soft ceiling panel, and exterior caulk crack. Photograph the roof from the ground if safe, and record approximate age of the last known reseal or coating. When comparing quotes, ask each company to state whether they will open any interior panels, replace wet decking if found, or only seal from above. A low price that ignores wet core is not a bargain. ## What a solid mobile roof visit usually includes

A thorough on-site package often includes: exterior walk-around and photo documentation; moisture readings at seams, penetrations, and stained interior locations; cleaning and priming of work areas; application of compatible sealants or coating per manufacturer directions; protection of sidewalls and equipment from drips; and a written summary of work performed plus product names. Ask for that package up front so you can compare apples to apples.

If the tech recommends a full recoating system, request the product data sheet, expected dry-film thickness or number of coats, walk-on cure time, and maintenance interval. Tucson UV is aggressive; coatings that look fine in milder climates can chalk or crack faster here if surface prep or film build is shorted.
