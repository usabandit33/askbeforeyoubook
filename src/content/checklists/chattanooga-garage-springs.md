A broken garage-door spring often sounds like a gunshot and can leave the door too heavy to open by hand. Homeowners want same-day help. The decision that matters is not only how fast someone arrives—it is whether both springs are replaced, whether the trip fee is credited, and who actually performs the work.

Springs work as a pair. Replacing only the broken one leaves the second spring near the end of its life and often means another emergency call later.

## 1. Ask whether both springs will be replaced

Most residential doors use two torsion springs (or a pair of extension springs). When one breaks, the other is usually the same age and cycle count.

Ask:

- Will you replace both springs?
- What is the price difference between one and both?
- Same size and rating for both?
- What warranty covers springs and labor?

A one-spring quote can look cheaper today and cost more when the second fails.

## 2. Confirm whether the trip or diagnostic fee is credited

Many companies charge a trip or diagnostic fee. Ask whether that amount applies toward the repair if you approve the work on the same visit. Get the answer on the estimate.

## 3. Ask who will perform the work

Some companies send employees; others dispatch independent techs or national call centers. Ask:

- Is the technician an employee of the company I am calling?
- Will the same person who quotes the job do the repair?
- Can I reach the company directly if there is a problem later?

Prefer operators that publish a local phone number and website over pure dispatch networks.

## 4. Clarify warranty and what is included

Ask what the warranty covers (springs, labor, or both), how long it lasts, and whether the price includes removing old springs, adjusting cables and balance, testing the door, and any hardware commonly replaced at the same time.
