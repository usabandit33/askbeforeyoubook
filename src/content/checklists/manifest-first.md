Operating a restaurant, commercial kitchen, bakery, or food truck commissary in Houston requires rigorous compliance with municipal Fats, Oils, and Grease (FOG) regulations. Under the City of Houston Code of Ordinances (Chapter 47, Article XI) and the Houston Health Department Environmental Waste Section, every food service establishment must maintain an approved grease trap or interceptor to prevent sewer blockages and street overflows into local bayous.

A common pitfall for new restaurant owners is hiring a cheap, unpermitted liquid waste hauler who "skims" only the top grease layer, fails to provide an official City of Houston manifest, or illegally dumps grease into city sewers. Under Texas environmental law, the food business that generated the grease remains legally liable for illicit dumping, facing municipal court citations and fines up to $2,000 per day.

Before scheduling grease trap pumping in Houston, use this checklist to ensure complete legal compliance and thorough trap cleaning.

## 1. Demand an official City of Houston Manifest (Trip Ticket) before the truck leaves

In Houston, paperwork is not optional; it is your primary legal defense during health inspections.

- **Ask:** *"Are you a permitted City of Houston Liquid Waste Transporter, and will you provide an official 5-part City of Houston / TCEQ manifest signed by the driver upon pump-out?"*
- **Legal requirement:** Houston city ordinances require every food establishment to retain completed, returned manifests on site for a minimum of **5 years**. When a Houston Health Department inspector arrives, they will demand to review your manifest binder.
- **Trip ticket cycle:** The manifest documents the date, time, total gallons pumped, generator signature, transporter permit number, and the state-permitted disposal facility where the grease was unloaded. If a hauler gives you a generic handwritten receipt instead of an official manifest, do not let them pump your trap.

## 2. Insist on a complete 100% pump-down, not top skimming

A legal, effective grease interceptor service must empty the tank completely.

- **Ask:** *"Do you pump the trap completely dry to the bottom, scrape the baffle walls, and inspect the sample well?"*
- **The skimming shortcut:** Shady pumpers insert a hose just 12 inches below the surface to vacuum the floating grease crust (the "scum" layer), leaving behind hundreds of gallons of rancid gray water and heavy settled food solids on the tank floor.
- **Why it matters:** The heavy solids ferment on the bottom, generating sulfuric acid that corrodes concrete interceptor walls and metal baffles. A thorough service involves pumping the tank completely dry, scraping grease off interior walls, vacuuming settled solids, and refilling with clean water to re-establish the hydraulic trap seal.

## 3. Understand the City of Houston 90-day / 25% FOG rule

Houston enforces strict minimum maintenance intervals regardless of business volume.

- **The 90-Day Rule:** Under city ordinance, all grease interceptors must be pumped completely empty at least once every **90 days**, unless a formal variance has been granted by the city health officer.
- **The 25% Rule:** If the combined thickness of the floating grease layer (scum) and bottom settled solids exceeds 25% of the total liquid depth of the trap, the trap must be pumped immediately, even if fewer than 90 days have elapsed. Once solids exceed 25%, grease begins bypassing the outlet baffle, spilling directly into the municipal sanitary sewer main.

## 4. Verify TCEQ transporter decals on the vacuum truck

In Texas, grease trap haulers must be registered with the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) and hold a valid City of Houston Transporter Permit.
- Look at the driver's truck: City of Houston permitted vehicles are required to display an active, annual city health department vehicle decal and TCEQ registration number on both sides of the truck cab.
- If the vehicle is unmarked or has out-of-state plates without Texas decals, do not allow them to hook up to your trap.

## 5. Inspect internal baffles and flow control devices

- Ask the technician to inspect the inlet and outlet sanitary tees and crossover baffles while the tank is empty.
- Missing, corroded, or collapsed PVC or cast-iron tees allow grease to escape into the city main, leading to automated city water surcharge penalties or emergency line blockage assessments.
