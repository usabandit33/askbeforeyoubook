Home Level 2 chargers can develop fault lights, thermal issues, or communication problems after a few years. Use these questions before you book a mobile diagnostic or repair visit in the Denver area.

## Questions to ask before you book

* **What fault codes or LED patterns is my unit showing?** Note any blinking sequences or color codes before the technician arrives; many manufacturers publish error tables (ChargePoint, Qmerit, and similar support guides).
* **Do you work on residential EVSE (Level 2 chargers) specifically?** Ask about experience with common brands, hardwired vs. NEMA plug units, and firmware updates.
* **Will you inspect for thermal stress on plugs and terminals?** Ask them to check for discoloration, burning odors, or excessive heat during high-amp sessions.
* **Can you test the dedicated 240V breaker and circuit?** Confirm they will verify ground-fault or thermal trip history and whether the circuit is still adequate.
* **Are you a licensed electrician for this work?** High-voltage EV supply equipment is not a DIY repair. Ask for license details and whether they carry the right diagnostic tools.
* **What is included in a mobile diagnostic visit?** Get the scope and price in writing—trip charge, diagnosis only vs. repair, parts availability, and any follow-up warranty.
* **Will you leave documentation of the fault and the work performed?** Keep records for warranty claims or future service.

## Why mobile EV charger service matters in Denver

Denver has seen rapid home EV adoption. Many Level 2 chargers installed in the last several years are now past their initial warranty window and are showing fault lights, repeated breaker trips, or loss of communication with the vehicle. Traditional electrical contractors are often booked with new construction and commercial work and treat residential charger service calls as low priority. A mobile technician who brings diagnostic tools to the garage can identify firmware issues, damaged cables, or circuit problems on site and restore charging without long wait times. Always use a licensed electrician for internal EVSE work; high-voltage equipment is not a homeowner repair job.
