Rapid suburban expansion into the Treasure Valley foothills has driven mice, squirrels, and other wildlife into residential attics and crawlspaces seeking shelter. Traditional pest control often relies on traps while leaving structural entry gaps open. Use these questions before you book permanent exclusion and remediation so you know the difference between a one-time catch and a lasting seal.

## Questions to ask before you book

* **Will you perform a full inspection of the roofline, soffits, gable vents, pipe penetrations, foundation, and crawlspace vents?** Entry points can be as small as a quarter-inch for mice. Ask how thoroughly they search, whether they map every gap, and whether they use cameras or other tools in tight spaces.
* **Do you seal with chew-proof materials (hardware cloth / steel mesh)?** Confirm they replace flimsy plastic screens and close gaps with materials rodents cannot chew through—steel mesh, metal flashing, or approved sealants—not expandable foam alone. Ask what gauge mesh they use and how they fasten it.
* **Do you address tree and vegetation access?** Ask whether they recommend or include trimming overhanging limbs (typically 6–8 feet from the roofline) that give squirrels and raccoons an easy path onto the roof, and whether vegetation work is in the written scope or a separate recommendation.
* **Is cleanup and sanitization of contaminated insulation included?** Droppings and pheromone trails attract new animals; ask what is included in the scope, whether framing is treated, how damaged insulation is handled, and whether they bag and remove contaminated material or leave that to the homeowner.
* **Is this exclusion work, or only trapping?** Clarify that the goal is permanent sealing of entry points, not just catch-and-release or monthly bait stations. City of Boise guidance emphasizes sealing openings and removing access and shelter; a trap-only plan leaves the house open to the next wave of animals.
* **Are you properly licensed and insured for this work?** Ask about Idaho State Department of Agriculture (ISDA) pest-control licensing when pesticides are used, and about liability coverage for attic, roof, and crawlspace work. Confirm they are equipped for confined, insulated spaces and that they carry the right credentials for the species involved.
* **What is the written scope and warranty?** Get the list of entry points sealed, materials used, crawlspace work included, and any follow-up guarantee in writing. Ask how long the warranty lasts and what conditions void it (for example, new openings from remodeling or storm damage).

## Securing Boise homes against attic and crawlspace wildlife

Boise’s mix of older neighborhoods, foothill edges, irrigation corridors, and new suburban growth creates steady pressure from mice, roof rats, squirrels, and occasional larger wildlife looking for warm shelter. Once animals establish nests in attics or dens in crawlspaces, they chew wiring, shred insulation, and leave contamination that can pose health risks. Homeowners often hear scratching at night, find droppings in storage areas, or notice damaged screens and vents long before they see the animals themselves.

A common frustration is the trap-only cycle: technicians catch the current animals but leave the structural openings that invited them in. General contractors often decline dusty, insulation-filled attic and crawlspace sealing work. Permanent exclusion—sealing every practical gap with chew-proof materials, addressing vegetation bridges, and cleaning as needed—stops the cycle rather than managing it month after month.

Local context that matters: the City of Boise does not provide extermination services and directs residents to licensed professionals for active infestations. Recent local ordinances emphasize reducing attractants and harborage (food, shelter, and access), including securing outdoor garbage, managing fallen fruit, and limiting exposed compost and debris. Central District Health and city guidance stress sealing cracks and gaps, screening vents with durable materials such as metal mesh, and using licensed help when rodents have reached areas that are difficult to clean safely. Always confirm current licensing with the Idaho State Department of Agriculture for pest-control applicators and follow local wildlife rules for protected species. Use licensed, insured technicians for attic and crawlspace work.

## Crawlspace and foundation details that often get skipped

Attic-focused quotes sometimes ignore foundation gaps, garage-door sweeps, and crawlspace vents that rodents use as alternate routes. Ask whether the inspection includes the full perimeter at grade, utility penetrations, and any attached structures. In older Boise Bench and North End homes, garage and foundation gaps are frequent entry points; in foothill and newer suburban homes, roofline and attic vents often dominate. A complete exclusion plan addresses both.

If you have an active infestation in insulation or ductwork, ask about cleanup standards and whether the company follows recognized guidance for handling droppings and contaminated materials (gloves, masks, wetting before sweeping, proper bagging). The City of Boise and Central District Health both point residents toward professional help when rodents have reached areas that are difficult to clean safely.
