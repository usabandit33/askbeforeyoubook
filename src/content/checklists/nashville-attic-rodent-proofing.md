Mice, rats, and squirrels readily enter Nashville attics through small gaps. The humid climate drives wildlife into residential attics seeking shelter. Traditional trapping alone often leaves the entry points open. Use these questions before you book a permanent exclusion service.

## Questions to ask before you book

* **Will you perform a full inspection of roofline, soffits, gable vents, and pipe penetrations?** Entry points can be as small as a dime for mice or a quarter for rats. Ask how thoroughly they search.
* **Do you seal with chew-proof materials (hardware cloth / steel mesh)?** Confirm they replace flimsy plastic screens and close gaps with materials rodents cannot chew through.
* **Do you address tree and vegetation access?** Ask whether they recommend or include trimming overhanging limbs (typically 6–8 feet from the roofline) that give squirrels and raccoons an easy path.
* **Is cleanup and sanitization of contaminated insulation included?** Pheromone trails and droppings can attract new animals; ask what is included in the scope and whether framing is treated.
* **Is this exclusion work, or only trapping?** Clarify that the goal is permanent sealing of entry points, not just catch-and-release or monthly bait stations.
* **Are you insured for attic and roof work?** Ask about liability coverage and whether they work in confined, insulated spaces.
* **What is the written scope and warranty?** Get the list of entry points sealed, materials used, and any follow-up guarantee in writing.

## Securing Nashville homes against wildlife infiltration

Nashville’s humid climate and tree cover make residential attics attractive to mice, rats, and squirrels. When seasonal temperature shifts occur, wildlife actively searches for warm, sheltered spaces and frequently finds its way into attics. Once inside, animals chew through electrical wiring, shred insulation, and create fire and health hazards.

A major frustration for homeowners is the traditional pest-control cycle: companies set traps to catch current invaders but fail to seal the structural entry points, leaving the home vulnerable to the next wave. Meanwhile, general home contractors often refuse to crawl through dusty, insulation-choked attics to perform meticulous exclusion work.

Specialized attic rodent proofing and exclusion services bridge this gap. By focusing on sealing every practical structural gap with chew-proof materials, cleaning and sanitizing as needed, and addressing access routes such as overhanging limbs, these experts stop infestations permanently rather than managing them month after month. Always confirm local wildlife and pest regulations and use licensed, insured technicians for attic work.
