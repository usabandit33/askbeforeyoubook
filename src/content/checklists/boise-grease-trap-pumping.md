## Questions to ask before you book

* **How do you apply the 25% rule?** Ask how the company measures combined FOG and settled solids against operating liquid depth and how that drives your pump schedule (common municipal FOG threshold; confirm with Boise Public Works / local FOG program).
* **Do you provide a signed waste manifest every visit?** Keep records on site. Ask about retention period and whether the hauler is licensed for liquid waste transport and disposal under local rules.
* **Is the service a full pump, clean, and inspect?** Confirm they remove all liquid and solids, clean walls and baffles, inspect sanitary tees and gaskets, and leave the interceptor empty and ready for use.
* **Are you licensed and permitted for commercial FOG hauling in Boise?** Ask for proof of approval or licensing that the city or health department will accept.
* **Do you recommend chemical emulsifiers or enzymes?** Many FOG programs prohibit or restrict products that liquefy grease so it can pass into the sewer. Confirm against current city guidance before agreeing.
* **What is the price per visit and the recommended interval?** Get it in writing, including emergency or after-hours rates if drains are already backing up.
* **How do you handle high-volume kitchens?** Ask for a schedule based on your seating, menu, and trap size rather than a one-size-fits-all interval.

## Staying on top of your grease trap in Boise

Boise’s restaurant scene has grown quickly. Every commercial kitchen sends fats, oils, and grease down the drain. An interceptor holds FOG back until the combined floating and settled layers take up too much of the operating depth—commonly treated as a 25% threshold that triggers cleaning. When the trap is overdue, grease can pass into the municipal line, harden, and cause backups or health violations. Standard plumbers often lack the vacuum equipment and disposal permits for this work, so dedicated liquid-waste haulers are the usual solution. Before you sign a contract, call the City of Boise Public Works or the Central District Health FOG contact to confirm current measurement rules, recordkeeping, and approved haulers—requirements can change.
