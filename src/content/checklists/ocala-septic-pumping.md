Rural and semi-rural properties around Ocala rely on septic systems that need periodic pumping and inspection. Sandy soils and older tanks make regular maintenance practical. Pumping removes sludge; inspection checks levels, baffles, and whether the drain field is the real problem. Doing one without the other can miss issues or pump a tank that did not yet need it.

## 1. Ask for inspect and measure—not only a pump

Before approving a pump-out, ask:

- Will sludge and scum levels be measured?
- Are inlet and outlet baffles checked?
- Is there any sign of drain-field surfacing or saturation?

An inspection-first approach can avoid unnecessary pumping and flag field problems that a pump alone will not fix.

## 2. Confirm pump scope, lids, and documentation

If pumping is recommended, get in writing:

- Whether the price covers a standard tank size or extra volume
- Whether lids or risers are opened, resealed, or replaced
- What written record or receipt you receive

Confirm the company is equipped for septic pumping and proper disposal in the area.

## 3. Compare inspection-only versus pump pricing

Ask for separate prices when possible, and whether an inspection fee is credited if you approve the pump on the same visit. Clarify recommended intervals for your household size and tank capacity.
