Clogged gutters can cause water to overflow and damage fascia, soffits, siding, and foundations. In Atlanta, leaf and debris buildup is common, especially after storms. The useful first step is a thorough cleaning. Guards or covers can reduce future buildup, but they do not eliminate the need for occasional maintenance, and they are not the right first purchase if the gutters are already full.

## 1. Clean the existing debris before adding guards

Gutters that already contain leaves, sticks, and sediment need to be cleaned before any guard is installed. Installing a cover over a clogged gutter traps the debris and can make later cleaning more difficult.

Ask the company:

- Will you clean the gutters completely before installing any guards?
- What method will you use to remove the debris and flush the downspouts?
- Do you inspect for damage, loose hangers, or improper slope while cleaning?

A cleaning-only visit is often the right first step. After the gutters are clear, you can decide whether guards are worth the cost for your trees and roof design.

## 2. Understand what guards do and do not prevent

Gutter guards can reduce the amount of debris that enters the gutter, but they are not maintenance-free. Fine particles, pine needles, and certain seeds can still pass through or collect on top of some designs.

Ask:

- What types of debris does this guard handle well?
- How often will the guards themselves need cleaning?
- What is the warranty, and what does it exclude?
- Will the installation affect roof or gutter warranties?

Compare the cost of the guards plus any expected future cleaning against the cost of periodic cleaning without guards.

## 3. Confirm the cleaning scope and price

A clear quote should state:

- Whether the price covers one story or multiple stories
- Whether downspouts are flushed
- Whether the company removes debris from the property or leaves it on site
- Any extra charge for heavy buildup or hard-to-reach sections

Get the answers in writing before scheduling.
