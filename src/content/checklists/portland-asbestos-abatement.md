If you are remodeling a Portland home built before 2004, asbestos planning belongs near the beginning of the project, not after a demolition crew has opened a wall. Asbestos-containing materials can be present in flooring, ceiling texture, insulation, siding, roofing, pipe wrap, and other building components. You cannot reliably identify asbestos by looking at a material. Disturbing it without the right survey, containment, work practices, and disposal can expose workers and occupants to hazardous fibers and create expensive delays.

Portland is a defensible market for this checklist because the local housing stock is old and the rules are unusually specific. The City of Portland's 2026–2030 Consolidated Plan, using 2020–2024 American Community Survey data, reports that 68.6% of owner-occupied homes in Multnomah County were built before 1980, including 37.0% before 1950. That is countywide housing data, not a count of homes containing asbestos. It does show why renovation-related hazardous-material planning is a recurring local concern.

Oregon DEQ says residential buildings built before January 1, 2004 generally need an asbestos survey by an accredited inspector before renovation or demolition. There is a narrow exception for an owner-occupant doing renovation work in their own single-family home. That exception does not apply simply because the property is a house: if you hire a contractor, are a landlord, or are preparing a residence for demolition, confirm the rule with DEQ before work begins.

## 1. Hire the right person for the survey, not just a contractor who offers a quick test

- **Ask:** *"Who will perform the asbestos survey, and what current accreditation qualifies that person to inspect and sample the materials in my project?"*
- **Request:** The inspector's name, accreditation or training credentials, laboratory information, and a written survey report identifying the areas inspected, materials sampled, limitations, and results.
- **Check:** Ask whether the inspector is independent of the removal contractor. If one company offers both services, ask how it separates the inspection findings from the sales estimate.

A proper report should do more than say "asbestos present" or "no asbestos found." Oregon DEQ's survey rules call for information about the facility's history and use, areas surveyed, inspector, survey limitations, response actions, and a table of identified asbestos-containing materials. Ask the inspector to explain any inaccessible areas or materials that were assumed rather than sampled.

Do not accept a visual guess as a laboratory result. If the report relies on samples, ask which accredited laboratory analyzed them and request the lab report with sample identifiers that match the survey.

## 2. Understand the difference between friable and nonfriable material

- **Ask:** *"Which materials are classified as friable, which are nonfriable, and what rule determines who may remove each one?"*
- **Request:** A room-by-room scope showing the material, location, quantity, condition, classification, proposed work method, and disposal plan.

Friable material can be crumbled by hand pressure and can release fibers more readily. Oregon DEQ says friable asbestos-containing material must be removed by a DEQ-licensed asbestos abatement contractor, subject to limited exceptions. Examples can include damaged pipe insulation or some sprayed and textured materials.

Nonfriable materials hold asbestos fibers in a more solid matrix, such as certain cement products or vinyl floor tiles. Oregon has separate requirements for nonfriable abatement, including advance notification to DEQ in applicable cases. DEQ says the ASN-6 notification and fee must be submitted at least five days before work begins. Do not assume that because a material is nonfriable, no rules apply.

A contractor should explain whether the proposed work is removal, repair, enclosure, or encapsulation. Ask why the chosen method is appropriate and what future work could disturb the material again.

## 3. Verify the removal contractor's Oregon credentials

- **Ask:** *"What is your current Oregon DEQ asbestos abatement contractor license number, and which certified supervisor will be on site?"*
- **Request:** A copy of the DEQ license, supervisor and worker credentials where applicable, current Oregon Construction Contractors Board (CCB) license, and proof of insurance.
- **Check:** Verify the contractor in DEQ's current asbestos contractor list and confirm the CCB license is active under the exact legal business name on your estimate.

Do not confuse an accredited asbestos inspector with an asbestos abatement contractor. They are different roles. Oregon DEQ requires licensing and certification for covered abatement work, while the CCB license is a separate contractor credential. A general remodeling license alone does not authorize a company to perform friable asbestos abatement.

If the proposal includes demolition, plumbing, electrical, roofing, or general remodeling beyond asbestos work, ask which licensed trade contractor is responsible for each part. Get subcontractor names and credentials in writing.

## 4. Compare containment, work methods, and disposal details

- **Ask:** *"How will you isolate the work area, control dust, protect HVAC openings, and prevent occupants from entering the work zone?"*
- **Request:** The written work plan, containment description, waste packaging and transport plan, disposal facility name, and any required DEQ notification records.

The proposal should describe how the work area will be separated from occupied spaces, how debris will be handled, and how the contractor will protect people who are not doing the work. Ask whether the HVAC system will be shut down or protected and how the crew will prevent contaminated dust from being tracked through the house.

Ask where the waste will go and what disposal documentation you will receive. Oregon DEQ regulates asbestos waste handling and disposal. A vague line that says "haul away debris" is not enough for a hazardous-material job.

## 5. Make the price and change-order rules specific

Asbestos bids can be difficult to compare because one contractor may price only the visible material while another includes survey coordination, containment, disposal, cleanup, and follow-up documentation.

- **Ask:** *"Does this price include the survey, laboratory analysis, DEQ notifications, containment, labor, disposal, cleanup, and required closeout records?"*
- **Ask:** *"What happens if concealed material is found after work begins, and will you stop and get written approval before adding charges?"*
- **Request:** A line-item estimate, project schedule, payment milestones, written change-order process, and warranty or workmanship terms.

Do not authorize demolition before the survey and required response plan are complete. If the scope changes, ask for an updated work plan and price before the contractor proceeds. Avoid paying the entire contract upfront.

## 6. Ask what documentation you will receive at the end

- **Ask:** *"What records will prove what was removed, what remains, where waste went, and whether the agreed work is complete?"*

Keep the full survey, laboratory reports, DEQ notifications, contractor license details, work plan, photographs, disposal receipts, invoices, and any clearance or post-work assessment that is appropriate for the project. Ask who will perform any post-abatement inspection or air monitoring, what method will be used, and whether that person is independent of the removal crew.

Do not assume a generic "certificate of asbestos removal" is a substitute for the actual records. Ask DEQ or your project designer what documentation applies to your specific material and scope.

## Warning signs

- The contractor says they can identify asbestos by sight alone.
- The company cannot identify the accredited inspector or provide the actual survey.
- A removal bid has no distinction between friable and nonfriable materials.
- The contractor cannot show the DEQ license required for the work being proposed.
- The estimate omits containment, waste handling, or disposal details.
- The crew wants to begin disturbing suspect material before the survey or required notification is addressed.
- The contractor promises that every asbestos project is "safe" without explaining the controls and documentation.

## Before you book

- Confirm the building's age and whether the project is renovation or demolition.
- Ask Oregon DEQ whether the survey requirement applies to your exact circumstances.
- Obtain a complete survey from an appropriately accredited inspector.
- Verify the abatement contractor's DEQ and CCB credentials.
- Compare written work plans, containment, disposal, price, and change-order terms.
- Confirm who handles DEQ notifications and any required permits.
- Keep the survey, lab results, notifications, disposal records, and closeout documents.

Portland city requirements and Oregon DEQ rules are not interchangeable. If your property is outside Portland, check the city or county building authority as well as DEQ. Rules can differ for owner-occupants, rental properties, multi-unit buildings, renovation, and demolition. Verify current requirements directly with Oregon DEQ and the local permitting office before authorizing work.
