A broken garage-door spring is a common emergency in Chicago. The door becomes heavy or impossible to lift by hand, and many homeowners want the repair done the same day. The decision that matters is not just how quickly someone can arrive. It is whether both springs will be replaced, whether the service call is credited, and who will actually perform the work.

Springs work as a pair. Replacing only the broken one often leaves the second spring near the end of its life. The next failure can come weeks or months later, requiring another service call.

## 1. Ask whether both springs will be replaced

Most residential garage doors use two torsion springs (or a pair of extension springs) that balance the door. When one breaks, the remaining spring is typically the same age and has the same cycle history.

Ask the company:

- Will you replace both springs?
- What is the price difference between replacing one and replacing both?
- Do you use the same spring size and rating for both?
- What warranty covers the springs and the labor?

A quote that replaces only the broken spring may look cheaper today. It often costs more later when the second spring fails.

## 2. Confirm whether the trip or diagnostic fee is credited

Many companies charge a trip fee or diagnostic fee. Ask whether that amount is applied toward the repair if you approve the work on the same visit.

Get the answer in writing or on the estimate. A company that credits the fee makes the total cost clearer. One that does not can add an unexpected charge after you have already agreed to the repair.

## 3. Ask who will perform the work

Some companies send their own employees. Others dispatch independent contractors. Ask:

- Is the technician an employee of the company I am calling?
- Will the same person who quotes the job perform the repair?
- Can I reach the company directly if there is a problem after the work is done?

A company that publishes its own phone number and website is usually easier to hold accountable than one that only appears through a lead service.

## 4. Understand the warranty and what is included

Ask what the warranty covers: the springs only, the labor, or both. Find out how long coverage lasts and whether it transfers if you sell the house.

Also confirm whether the price includes:

- Removing and disposing of the old springs
- Adjusting the door balance and cables
- Testing the door after the repair
- Any additional hardware that is commonly replaced at the same time

## Before you book

Use the matching Chicago garage-door spring checklist to confirm both springs, the trip-fee credit, and who will show up. Get the scope and warranty in writing before authorizing the repair.
