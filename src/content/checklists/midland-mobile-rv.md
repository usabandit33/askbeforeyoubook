RVs in the Permian Basin often sit on oilfield lots, remote sites, or storage yards where towing to a dealership bay is slow and expensive. Mobile technicians bring tools and parts to the unit. The right choice depends on the type of repair, parts availability, and whether the rig can be moved safely.

## 1. Decide whether the repair needs a shop bay

Ask the company:

- Can this issue be diagnosed and fixed on-site with the tools they carry?
- Will major components (slide mechanisms, generators, roof sections) require a controlled environment or lift?
- What happens if the first visit cannot finish the job?

Mobile service works well for many appliance, electrical, plumbing, and seal repairs. Complex chassis or structural work may still need a shop.

## 2. Clarify travel fees, response time, and parts

Get in writing:

- Base service call or first-hour rate and any mileage charge
- Typical arrival window for Midland, Odessa, and nearby sites
- Whether common parts are stocked on the truck or ordered after diagnosis
- Emergency or after-hours rates if the unit is stranded

Confirm whether the quote is for diagnosis only or includes labor once the problem is known.

## 3. Verify credentials and insurance

Ask whether the technicians are certified (RVIA, manufacturer training, or equivalent) and whether the company carries liability and workers’ compensation coverage for work performed at your location.
