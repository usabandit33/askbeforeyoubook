Lanai and pool screen enclosures across Central Florida—from Orlando and Winter Park to Kissimmee and Lake Mary—take a constant beating from intense ultraviolet sun, high summer humidity, and tropical storm wind gusts. Over time, standard fiberglass screening turns brittle and tears like paper, while galvanic corrosion eats away at the steel fasteners holding the aluminum frame to the concrete pool deck.

When a storm blows out several screen panels, homeowners face a critical decision: Is this a simple panel rescreening job, or has structural aluminum corrosion compromised the integrity of the enclosure?

Before hiring a screen enclosure contractor in Orlando, use this checklist to separate surface screen fixes from vital structural frame reinforcement.

## 1. Differentiate individual panel replacement from a full rescreen

Replacing two or three torn panels is quick, but can be a waste of money if the entire enclosure is aged.

- **The pinch test:** Gently pinch a section of screen between your thumb and forefinger on an undamaged panel. If the fiberglass screen feels stiff, dry, or powdery, or tears with minimal pressure, the UV inhibitors have completely broken down.
- **Ask:** *"What is the cost to replace individual damaged panels versus a full enclosure rescreen with new heavy-duty rubber spline?"*
- **Why it matters:** If an enclosure is 8 to 12 years old, replacing 4 panels today often means 4 different panels will tear during the next thunderstorm. A full rescreen includes replacing the aged vinyl spline cord in every channel and resetting the tension across the entire roof and wall structure.

## 2. Inspect for rusted fasteners and galvanic corrosion

The #1 cause of catastrophic pool cage collapse during Florida hurricanes is not broken aluminum beams, but rusted screws.

- **Ask:** *"Will your technician inspect the frame fasteners, and do you replace rusted exterior screws with 304/316 stainless steel or ceramic-coated Nylotec screws?"*
- **The science:** Builders often assemble pool cages using cheap carbon steel screws. When bare steel touches aluminum in a wet environment, galvanic corrosion occurs: the steel rusts, expands, and snaps off inside the aluminum extrusion.
- **The danger:** If the base screws securing the bottom aluminum footer to your concrete patio are rusted away, a modest 60 mph gust can lift and twist the entire structure into the pool.

## 3. Choose the right mesh material for Central Florida bugs and sun

Not all screening material is the same:
- **Standard 18x14 Fiberglass Mesh:** The standard builder grade. Economical, but small midges and gnats ("No-See-Ums") can easily walk through the openings.
- **20x20 No-See-Um Mesh:** Tighter weave designed specifically to keep out tiny biting insects and filter out blowing pine needles and oak pollen.
- **Polyester Screen (TuffScreen / Super Screen):** Vinyl-coated polyester yarn with tear resistance 3 to 4 times greater than fiberglass. Backed by 10-year manufacturer warranties, it withstands hail, falling tree twigs, and climbing pets.

## 4. Inspect hurricane cables and diagonal wind braces

Under the Florida Building Code (Chapter 20 - Aluminum Structures), screened pool enclosures are engineered to withstand specific regional wind loads (typically 120–140 mph design speeds in Orange and Osceola counties).

- **Ask:** *"Are the corner diagonal wind braces and stainless steel tension cables properly tensioned, and are any aluminum purlins bent or loose?"*
- Loose or missing wind cables cause the frame to rack (twist) during squalls, popping out screen spline and loosening roof joints.

## 5. Verify Florida licensing and workers' compensation insurance

Working on pool cage roofs is hazardous.
- In Florida, screen enclosure construction and structural repair requires a licensed Specialty Structure Contractor (SCC), Certified Building Contractor (CBC), or General Contractor (CGC).
- Verify active licensing and workers’ compensation coverage on the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) portal. If an uninsured handyman falls through your pool enclosure roof, you could be held liable.
