Main-line and sewer clogs in the Pensacola area are often caused by grease, roots, scale, or debris. A cable (snake) can punch a hole through a blockage. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the pipe walls and remove buildup more thoroughly. The right method depends on the pipe material, the nature of the clog, and whether a camera inspection has been done.

## 1. Start with a camera inspection when possible

Ask whether the company will run a sewer camera before or after cleaning. A camera helps:

- Locate the blockage and identify roots, offsets, or collapsed sections
- Confirm the pipe is sound enough for high-pressure jetting
- Document the condition before and after the work

Without a camera, jetting may clear a soft clog but miss structural problems.

## 2. Understand snake versus hydro jetting

- Cable snaking is usually faster and less expensive for simple stoppages.
- Hydro jetting uses pressurized water to clean grease, scale, and soft root intrusion from the pipe interior.
- Jetting is not appropriate for severely damaged or certain older pipe materials.

Ask what pressure and nozzle type will be used and whether the company carries both options.

## 3. Clarify pricing, access, and follow-up

Get in writing:

- Whether the price is for the first hour or a complete main-line clean
- Any extra charges for difficult access, clean-outs, or after-hours work
- Whether a post-jet camera video is included
- Recommendations if the line needs repair rather than cleaning
