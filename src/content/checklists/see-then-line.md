When drains back up in a Chicago bungalow, two-flat, or brownstone, panicked homeowners are often told their main sewer lateral has collapsed and requires a $10,000 to $25,000 emergency excavation. In many cases, the pipe is simply clogged with mature tree roots, grease, or scale—or has a localized crack that can be repaired with targeted trenchless technology.

Chicago homes typically connect to the city main via vitrified clay tile sewer pipes laid decades ago. Before authorizing an expensive dig in your yard, parkway, or basement floor, follow this step-by-step diagnostic checklist.

## 1. Always demand a high-definition camera inspection with recorded video

Never authorize major sewer excavation or lining based on a plumber's verbal description. Modern sewer diagnosis requires a full-length camera run.

- **Ask:** *"Will you provide a digital video file (flash drive or cloud link) of the complete camera run, including footage markers showing the exact distance of the defect from the cleanout?"*
- **Inspect the screen yourself:** Ask the technician to point out whether they see tree root intrusion at the clay pipe joints, a bellied (sagging) section holding standing water, or an actual crushed/collapsed pipe wall.
- **Red flag:** A contractor who refuses to provide a copy of the video inspection or claims their camera *"doesn't record."* Always get a second opinion with your recorded video before digging.

## 2. Jet first before declaring a pipe unlineable

A mechanical drain snake (auger) punches a narrow 2-to-3-inch hole through grease or roots to restore temporary flow, but it cannot clean the pipe walls. 

- **Ask:** *"Can we run a high-pressure hydro jetter to clear roots and sediment before we make a final repair decision?"*
- **Why it matters:** Hydro jetting scours the interior circumference of the clay pipe with 3,000+ PSI water streams. Once the pipe is thoroughly scrubbed, re-inserting the camera often reveals that what looked like a shattered pipe was merely heavy calcite buildup or temporary root clumps.

## 3. Compare CIPP trenchless lining vs. spot repair vs. open trench dig

Depending on the pipe's condition and location, you have distinct repair options:
- **Cured-in-Place Pipe (CIPP) Lining:** An epoxy-saturated felt tube is inverted into the existing clay pipe and cured into a seamless "pipe within a pipe." This avoids tearing up finished landscaping, driveways, or basement concrete slabs.
- **Point (Spot) Repair:** If 90% of the pipe is structurally sound and only one joint near a tree has failed, excavating a small 4x4 hole for a localized PVC repair is far cheaper than replacing the entire lateral.
- **Traditional Open Dig:** Required if the pipe has completely collapsed, has negative slope (a belly), or is sheared off.

## 4. Clarify property line limits and City of Chicago permits

In Chicago, homeowners are generally responsible for the private sewer lateral from the house out to the city main connection, even where it passes beneath the public sidewalk and parkway.
- **Ask:** *"Does the repair require opening the public parkway or street? Does your company hold the required Chicago Department of Transportation (CDOT) public right-of-way permit and Department of Water Management (DWM) drainlayer license?"*
- Only licensed and bonded Chicago Drainlayers are legally authorized to perform sewer excavations in the city. Confirm their license status before signing.

## 5. Verify warranties and backflow prevention

Basement sewer backups are common during Chicago heavy rainstorms due to combined sewer overload.
- **Ask:** *"What is the warranty on materials and labor? Does your lining carry a 25-to-50-year manufacturer warranty?"*
- Consider asking the plumber about installing a flood control system, overhead sewer conversion, or check valve (backwater preventer) to protect your basement from municipal sewer surcharges during lake-effect storms.
