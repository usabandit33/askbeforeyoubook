## Questions to ask before you book

* **What fault codes or LED patterns is my unit showing?** Note any blinking sequences or color-coded LEDs before resetting the breaker or the technician arrives. Many manufacturers publish error tables (ChargePoint, Qmerit, and similar support guides).
* **Do you work on residential EVSE (Level 2 chargers) specifically?** Ask about experience with common brands, hardwired vs. NEMA plug units, firmware updates, and thermal-trip diagnostics common on aging Silicon Valley installations.
* **Will you inspect for thermal stress on plugs and terminals?** Ask them to check NEMA plugs or hardwired terminals for discoloration, burning odors, or excessive heat during high-amp charging sessions.
* **Can you test the dedicated 240V breaker and circuit?** Confirm they will verify ground-fault or thermal trip history and whether the circuit still meets continuous-load rules under California Electrical Code Article 625 (typically sized at 125% of the EVSE maximum load).
* **Are you a licensed C-10 electrician for this work?** High-voltage EV supply equipment is not a DIY repair. In San Jose, Level 2 work is generally performed by a California C-10 (Electrical) contractor; ask for license details and whether they carry the right diagnostic tools for EVSE.
* **Will any repair or replacement trigger a permit?** Hardwired Level 2 installs and many circuit changes require an electrical permit through San José Online Permit Center (SJPermits.org). Ask whether the planned work needs a permit or inspection and who will pull it.
* **What is included in a mobile diagnostic visit?** Get the scope and price in writing—trip charge, diagnosis only vs. repair, parts availability, and any follow-up warranty. Ask for documentation of the fault and the work performed for warranty claims.
