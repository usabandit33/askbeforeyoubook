# Atlanta Commercial Hood Cleaning: What a Complete Grease-Duct Cleaning Includes

A commercial kitchen exhaust system does more than remove smoke and cooking odors. Grease can accumulate on filters, inside the hood, in exhaust ductwork, and on the exhaust fan. If cleaning only covers the visible parts, grease may remain in less accessible sections of the system.

For Atlanta restaurant owners and kitchen managers, the key question is not simply whether a company offers "hood cleaning." It is which parts of the exhaust system will actually be cleaned, and how will the work be documented?

## 1. Understand the difference between a filter wash and a full system cleaning

A filter wash removes grease from the removable filters. That is useful, but it does not clean the hood plenum, the ductwork, or the exhaust fan.

A complete cleaning reaches the interior surfaces where grease collects. Ask whether the service includes:

- Hood interior and plenum
- Exhaust ductwork
- Exhaust fan and housing
- Accessible grease containment devices

If the quote only mentions filters or a "light cleaning," clarify the full scope before scheduling.

## 2. Ask how access is handled

Duct cleaning often requires opening access panels or creating temporary openings. Confirm who is responsible for access, whether any building modifications are needed, and whether the quote includes restoring panels or surfaces afterward.

## 3. Request documentation of the cleaning

Ask for a report or certificate that lists the areas cleaned, the date, and any limitations. Some jurisdictions or insurers expect records of commercial kitchen exhaust cleaning.
