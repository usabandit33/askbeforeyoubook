Commercial properties in Huntsville collect dirt, algae, and stains on sidewalks, storefronts, parking areas, and suitable building exteriors. Humidity and traffic make routine exterior cleaning a practical maintenance item for property managers. The useful questions are which surfaces will be cleaned, how methods match each material, and whether the schedule works around tenants and customers.

## 1. Specify surfaces—not a vague whole-property clean

Identify sidewalks, entrances, storefronts, parking or loading zones, and facades separately. Ask how stained concrete will be treated and whether high pressure is appropriate for brick, paint, or siding. Matching method to surface reduces damage risk.

## 2. Confirm commercial experience, insurance, and runoff practices

Ask for comparable commercial work (retail, office, industrial). Request proof of liability insurance and applicable workers’ compensation. Discuss pedestrian safety, equipment placement, and how wastewater and detergents are managed relative to local storm-drain expectations.

## 3. Get an itemized quote and a workable schedule

A useful proposal lists surfaces, method, estimated timing, price, and exclusions (stain treatment, after-hours, water access). For busy sites, confirm early-morning, overnight, or weekend options and how areas stay safe until dry.

Ask for separate pricing if you want a recurring maintenance plan versus a one-time clean, and clarify cancellation and extra-work terms before signing a long agreement.
