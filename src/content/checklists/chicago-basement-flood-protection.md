Chicago homeowners often hear several different fixes for the same wet basement: a sump pump, a backwater valve, an overhead sewer, downspout disconnection, exterior drainage work, or a full excavation. Those solutions address different failure points. The right first step is a documented diagnosis, not a one-size-fits-all package.

The City of Chicago says basement flooding can occur when sewers fill during heavy rain and recommends keeping roof water out of the sewer system. The city also warns that nobody can guarantee a home will never flood. That is a useful truth to keep in front of you when a salesperson promises a permanent miracle.

## 1. Identify the water source before buying equipment

Ask:

- "Was the water rainwater, groundwater, sewage, or a failed plumbing fixture?"
- "What evidence proves the proposed fix addresses that source?"
- "Did you inspect the floor drain, sewer lateral, downspouts, sump basin, and foundation walls?"
- "Can you show photographs or moisture readings?"

Request a written diagnosis with the date, locations inspected, and limits of the inspection. A basement waterproofing quote based only on a salesman's visual walk-through is incomplete. Water can enter through a foundation crack, a blocked drain, a failed ejector, a surcharge in the public sewer, or a downspout discharge pointed at the foundation.

## 2. Separate sewer-backup protection from foundation drainage

A backwater valve or overhead sewer may help prevent sewage from moving backward through a drain. A sump system handles groundwater or collected drainage. Downspout disconnection keeps roof runoff out of the combined sewer system. Exterior grading, gutters, and foundation drainage address yet another pathway.

Ask the contractor to state which problem each component solves and which problems it does not solve. Request a simple diagram showing the water path. If the proposal includes a valve, ask where it will be installed, how it will be accessed for cleaning, and who will maintain it.

## 3. Verify the Chicago license and permit path

Chicago requires a licensed and bonded plumbing contractor for most plumbing work. The city's plumbing guidance also points homeowners to licensed trade contractors and explains that drainlayer licensing can matter for sewer construction, repair, and cleaning.

Ask:

- "What City of Chicago license authorizes this exact work?"
- "Who pulls the plumbing, drainlayer, excavation, or public-way permits?"
- "Will any work extend beyond the foundation or into the parkway?"
- "Who schedules inspection and restores concrete, landscaping, or pavement?"

Request the license number, insurance certificate, bond information when applicable, permit number, and inspection plan. Do not accept "the city does not care about permits" as an answer. The buried part of the job is precisely the part that becomes hard to verify later.

## 4. Compare the whole system, not one shiny component

A quote should identify pumps, battery backup, alarm, discharge route, check valves, backwater protection, electrical work, excavation, patching, and follow-up service. Ask what happens during a power outage. A single primary pump without a tested backup plan may leave you with a new receipt and the same old panic.

For downspouts, Chicago advises directing discharge away from foundations and neighboring property. Ask where the water will go in winter, whether icing is possible, and whether the route could create a new seepage problem.

## 5. Clarify warranties and maintenance

Ask:

- "What is covered for labor, pumps, valves, alarms, and battery systems?"
- "Who tests the system after installation?"
- "How often should the pump and valve be serviced?"
- "What happens if the discharge line freezes or clogs?"
- "Does the warranty exclude storm events or municipal sewer surcharge?"

A contractor who sells a lifetime system but cannot explain maintenance is selling a slogan. Keep the manuals, permit, inspection sign-off, photographs, and service schedule with your property records.

## Warning signs

- The contractor recommends the same package for every basement.
- There is no written diagnosis or water-path explanation.
- The proposal excludes permits, electrical work, discharge routing, or restoration.
- The company will not provide a Chicago license number.
- The quote promises the basement can never flood again.
- A large deposit is demanded before inspection or a written scope.
- The contractor pressures you to sign during the first visit.

## Before you book

Get an address-specific diagnosis, verify the contractor's Chicago license and insurance, compare at least three itemized bids, and confirm who handles permits and inspections. Ask the City of Chicago or your water and sewer authority whether a public-main issue or assistance program may affect the repair. Verify current local requirements before signing because permit rules and program details can change.
