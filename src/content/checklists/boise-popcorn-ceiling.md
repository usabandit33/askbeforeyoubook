Popcorn (acoustic) ceilings were common in homes built through the 1970s and early 1980s. Many contain asbestos. Disturbing the material without testing can create a health hazard and complicate disposal. The first decision is whether the ceiling needs testing; the second is whether removal and a new finish are worth the cost and mess.

## 1. Test before any scraping or sanding

If the home or texture dates before roughly 1980, ask the company:

- Will a sample be collected and sent to a lab before work begins?
- Who pays for the test and how long does the result take?
- What happens if asbestos is confirmed (licensed abatement vs containment methods)?

Do not allow wet scraping or dry sanding of untested material in older homes.

## 2. Clarify the removal and finish scope

Ask for a written description of:

- Containment and dust control methods
- How much of the texture will be removed versus skim-coated
- Whether drywall repairs, tape, or texture matching are included
- The final finish (smooth, light texture, or paint-ready)

Get square-footage pricing and any extra charges for high ceilings or repairs.

## 3. Compare cost, timeline, and cleanup

Confirm how many days the job is expected to take, who handles debris disposal, and what the final cleaning includes. A lower bid may skip testing, proper containment, or a quality finish coat.
