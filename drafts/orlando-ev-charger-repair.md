An EV charger that stops charging can have several causes: the vehicle, charging cable, wall connector, circuit, breaker, or the home's available electrical capacity. In Orlando, the right repair company should be able to diagnose the complete charging setup rather than immediately selling a replacement charger. The City of Orlando's EV Readiness Guide recommends a qualified electrical contractor who can assess service capacity and perform load calculations for EV charging equipment. Its permitting guidance also says EV charging station projects require an electrical permit with an associated engineering permit issued to a licensed general or electrical contractor. Those are useful standards even when you are hiring someone to troubleshoot an existing installation.

## 1. Are you licensed for the electrical work you are proposing?

- **Ask:** *"What Florida contractor license covers this repair, and will the licensed contractor personally supervise the electrical work?"*
- **Red flag:** A company that treats panel, circuit, wiring, or hardwired charger work as ordinary handyman work.

Florida's Department of Business and Professional Regulation explains that electrical contractors install, repair, alter, add to, or design electrical wiring, fixtures, or appliances for compensation. Ask for the contractor's legal business name and license number, then verify the license through the state's official licensing portal. If the problem is only a software setting or a manufacturer warranty issue, the scope may be different. But do not let a company blur the line between app support and paid electrical repair.

## 2. Will you diagnose the vehicle, charger, and circuit before recommending replacement?

- **Ask:** *"What tests will you perform to determine whether the fault is the vehicle, cable, charger, breaker, wiring, or supply?"*
- **Red flag:** A replacement quote based only on a flashing light or a brief visual inspection.

Before the appointment, write down the charger brand and model, whether it is hardwired or plugged into a receptacle, the error code or light pattern, when the issue began, and whether charging works at another location. Share photos of the charger's label and any visible damage without opening the equipment. Ask the technician to document the diagnosis and explain which test supports the recommended repair. If the charger is still under warranty, ask whether the manufacturer requires an authorized service provider or a case number before work begins.

## 3. Will you check the electrical capacity and installation details?

- **Ask:** *"Will you verify the circuit rating, breaker, conductor size, charger settings, and available service capacity before changing anything?"*
- **Red flag:** A promise to install a larger breaker or increase charging amperage without checking the entire circuit.

A charger should not be made to draw more current simply because the vehicle is charging slowly. The City of Orlando's EV Readiness Guide recommends a site assessment and load calculation to determine service capacity and power needs. Ask the contractor to explain whether the existing circuit and panel are suitable for the charger's configured output. If upgrades are needed, request a written scope identifying the proposed circuit, equipment, permit, inspection, and any utility coordination.

## 4. Who handles permits, inspections, and manufacturer requirements?

- **Ask:** *"Does this repair require a permit or inspection, and will you handle the application and closeout?"*
- **Red flag:** A contractor who tells you permits never apply to EV charging work.

Orlando's permit page says an EV charging station installation requires an electrical permit with an associated engineering permit issued to a licensed general or electrical contractor. The city also describes required plans and a Notice of Commencement for projects above its stated value threshold. A like-for-like component repair may be treated differently from a new circuit, relocation, or capacity upgrade, so ask the contractor to confirm the requirement with the city for your exact scope. Get the permit number when one is required and keep the final inspection record with your home documents.

## 5. What will the written estimate and warranty cover?

- **Ask:** *"Will the estimate separate diagnosis, parts, labor, permit fees, and any optional upgrades?"*
- **Red flag:** A verbal price that changes after the charger is opened or a replacement is installed without your approval.

Ask whether the diagnostic fee is credited toward an approved repair, whether replacement parts are new or refurbished, and who backs the labor warranty. If the charger itself has failed, compare the repair price with the cost of a new compatible unit, including installation and permit work. Ask the contractor to return the old component or show you the failed part when practical. Do not pay for an upgrade unless the reason is documented and you agree to it.

## Before you book

Choose a Florida-licensed electrical contractor for wiring, breaker, panel, or hardwired-charger work. Provide the model, symptoms, and installation details before the visit. Require a diagnosis before replacement, a written estimate, and confirmation about permits and inspections. Do not open the charger, remove a panel cover, or touch exposed wiring yourself. A non-contact voltage tester is not a substitute for a qualified electrician's test equipment or safe work practices.
