Window replacement is a classic example of a home project that can look simple in an advertisement and become complicated once the salesperson starts talking about energy savings, financing, trim, rot, permits, and "today only" pricing. The real cost depends on the opening, the frame condition, installation method, flashing, interior and exterior finish work, and whether the quote includes disposal and repairs.

Detroit is a promising market for this checklist because Michigan's Attorney General has recently warned that complaints about home-improvement and window-replacement offers include high-pressure sales, confusing contract terms, poor workmanship, failure to honor warranties, incomplete work, overcharging, and threats of liens. In March 2026, the Attorney General reported that the Consumer Protection Team received more than 12,000 written complaints in 2025 and recovered about $1.88 million for Michigan consumers and businesses through mediation, settlements, and refunds. Those figures cover the state and many industries, not Detroit window contractors specifically. They still show why written scope and verification matter.

Detroit also requires permits for many construction and system changes, and the city's permit guidance says renovation and replacement work may require permits and inspections. Confirm the exact requirement for your property and scope rather than accepting a salesperson's blanket statement that windows are "permit free."

## 1. Identify the contractor and licensing path

- **Ask:** *"What is the exact legal business name, and which Michigan license or local registration applies to this window project?"*
- **Request:** Proof of insurance, workers' compensation coverage, subcontractor names, and the person responsible for the permit.
- **Check:** Verify the contractor with Michigan's licensing and construction authorities and confirm whether the work requires a building permit.

Michigan's own consumer guidance tells homeowners to research the contractor, obtain required permits, and tie payments to completed work. The Attorney General also warns against pressure to sign quickly. A company may use a national brand, a local salesperson, and a separate installation subcontractor, so make sure the contract names the actual entity receiving your money and doing the work.

If the project includes structural opening changes, masonry, lead-safe work, or exterior repairs, ask which licensed professional handles each part. A general sales representative is not proof that the installer is qualified.

## 2. Make the quote define the product and installation method

- **Ask:** *"What exact window model, glass package, frame material, dimensions, and installation method are included?"*
- **Request:** Manufacturer data sheets, U-factor and solar-heat-gain information, warranty documents, and a room-by-room schedule.

A useful quote should identify whether the job is full-frame replacement or insert replacement. It should state the number of windows, rough-opening measurements, sill and jamb repairs, flashing, insulation, trim, caulk, painting, screens, disposal, and cleanup.

Be skeptical of vague energy-saving promises. Ask whether the quoted values apply to the whole unit or only the glass, and whether the contractor is using certified performance data. Do not assume a lower U-factor automatically means a lower total energy bill. Air leakage, installation quality, shading, and the rest of the building envelope matter too.

## 3. Ask how hidden damage will be handled

- **Ask:** *"What happens if you find rot, water damage, lead paint, masonry failure, or an opening that does not match the estimate?"*
- **Request:** A written change-order process, photographs, and a price before extra work begins.

Older Detroit homes may have damaged sills, deteriorated brick, uneven openings, or layers of previous repairs. A window quote that is cheap because it assumes perfect framing may become much more expensive after demolition.

Require the contractor to stop and document concealed conditions. Do not authorize open-ended "time and materials" work without a ceiling, written scope, or approval process.

## 4. Confirm permits, inspections, and lead-safe responsibilities

- **Ask:** *"Who will obtain the permit, schedule inspections, and handle any lead-safe requirements that apply to this property?"*
- **Request:** Permit number, inspection plan, and any lead-safe certification or project documentation required for the home's age and scope.

Detroit says permits may be required for construction, renovation, and changes to building systems. The city's permit pages also emphasize that permit records and inspections matter. If the home is older, ask whether the work disturbs painted surfaces and whether federal or state lead-safe rules apply. Do not accept a vague statement that the contractor "does lead" without seeing the relevant credential or work plan.

## 5. Put payment, cancellation, and warranty terms in writing

- **Ask:** *"How much is due at signing, when are progress payments due, and what labor warranty applies if a window leaks or operates poorly?"*
- **Request:** Complete contract, financing disclosures if any, cancellation notice, manufacturer warranty, installation warranty, and lien-waiver process.

Michigan's Attorney General warns about overcharging, incomplete work, warranty failures, and lien threats. Keep payments tied to measurable progress. Ask what happens if windows are delayed, damaged in delivery, or installed with visible defects. If financing is offered, read the financing contract separately from the installation agreement.

Do not sign a blank contract or accept a same-day discount that disappears when you ask for time to compare bids.

## Warning signs

- The salesperson refuses to identify the actual installer or legal contracting entity.
- Energy savings are promised without product data or assumptions.
- The quote omits flashing, trim, disposal, or hidden-damage procedures.
- Permit and inspection responsibility is unclear.
- The contractor demands full payment before delivery or installation.
- The company pressures you to sign immediately or discourages a second quote.
- Warranty terms are verbal, incomplete, or split between multiple companies with no clear contact.

## Before you book

- Get at least three written bids using the same window type and scope.
- Verify the business name, insurance, licensing, and subcontractors.
- Confirm Detroit permit and inspection requirements for your address.
- Compare whole-window performance data, not just marketing claims.
- Put rot, masonry, lead-safe work, trim, disposal, and change orders in writing.
- Keep the installation and manufacturer warranties together.
- Do not let urgency replace due diligence.

If the property is outside Detroit, local permit, historic-district, and contractor rules may differ. Verify current requirements with the local building department and Michigan licensing authorities before signing.
