Residential solar arrays installed across Melbourne, Suntree, West Melbourne, and the rest of Brevard during earlier incentive waves are aging. Summer heat, coastal humidity, and lightning storms stress inverters and microinverters. Many original installers are focused on new construction or commercial bids and slow to respond to out-of-warranty service calls. Homeowners left with underproducing arrays need a clear diagnostic path—not a rush to replace the entire system.

Before you book, decide whether the problem is a single microinverter, a string inverter, communication gear, or something elsewhere in the array. Get that distinction in writing.

## 1. Will you diagnose before recommending replacement?

- **Ask:** *"What tests will you run to determine whether the fault is a microinverter, string inverter, monitoring gateway, wiring, or the panels themselves?"*
- **Red flag:** A quote to replace the inverter based only on an app alert or a brief visual look.

Enphase-style microinverter systems can lose one or a few modules while the rest of the array still produces. String inverters can take down a whole string. Monitoring history, device status codes, and on-site measurements matter more than a single error message. Ask the company to document the diagnosis and explain which test supports the recommended repair.

## 2. Are you licensed for the electrical and solar work you propose?

- **Ask:** *"What Florida contractor license covers this diagnostic and repair work, and will a licensed person supervise the electrical portion?"*
- **Red flag:** Treating hardwired inverter or AC-disconnect work as ordinary handyman service.

Inverter and microinverter work often involves AC and DC electrical connections. Florida regulates electrical contracting. Ask for the legal business name and license number and verify them through the state licensing portal before work begins. Software resets or manufacturer warranty coordination may not require the same scope, but panel, disconnect, and hardwired equipment work does.

## 3. Who handles warranty claims versus out-of-warranty service?

- **Ask:** *"If any component is still under manufacturer warranty, will you document the failure and submit or support the claim, or is this strictly out-of-warranty labor?"*
- **Red flag:** A company that refuses to discuss warranty documentation when the unit age suggests coverage may still apply.

Some microinverter lines carry long manufacturer warranties, but claims typically need diagnostic evidence from a qualified service provider. Confirm whether the company will produce the paperwork you need or whether you must coordinate with the manufacturer yourself. Get the labor and parts portions of the quote separated so you can see what is warranty-covered and what is not.

## 4. What is included in the service visit and the written estimate?

- **Ask:** *"Does the diagnostic fee credit toward approved repair work, and will the estimate separate diagnosis, parts, labor, roof access, and any permit fees?"*
- **Red flag:** A verbal price that changes after equipment is opened, without photos or a written change order.

Provide the system brand, approximate install year, monitoring app screenshots, and whether production dropped suddenly or gradually. Ask whether roof access, ladder fees, or multiple site visits are included. If several microinverters have failed, ask whether the company will prioritize the worst units first and leave a clear punch list for follow-up.

## 5. Safety and scope boundaries

- **Ask:** *"Will you work only within your licensed scope, and will you stop and show me any condition that needs a different trade or the utility?"*
- **Red flag:** Opening panels, working in the main service panel, or promising grid-interconnection changes without the right license or utility coordination.

Do not open inverter enclosures, DC combiners, or the main electrical panel yourself. Burning smells, charring, exposed conductors, or ground-fault warnings deserve prompt professional attention—not DIY probing. A non-contact voltage tester is not a substitute for a qualified electrician’s procedures.

## Before you book

Confirm licensing, require a documented diagnosis before replacement, clarify warranty versus paid labor, and get a written estimate that separates parts and labor. Melbourne-area systems face heat and storm stress; a careful diagnostic visit is usually cheaper than replacing the wrong component.
