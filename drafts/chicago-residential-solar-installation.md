A rooftop solar proposal can look simple: a monthly payment, an estimated electric-bill reduction, and a promise that the system will pay for itself. The actual deal can involve a long financing term, dealer fees, production assumptions, utility approvals, roof work, warranties, and program paperwork. Those details matter more than the salesperson's savings graphic.

Illinois publishes unusually useful consumer-protection information for solar shoppers. The Illinois Power Agency's 2025 consumer complaints report, revised in April 2026, recorded 832 complaints in the Illinois Shines and Illinois Solar for All programs. The most frequent primary complaint category was mechanical or installation issues, with 228 complaints. The report also recorded 199 complaints about installation-contract or financing terms and 143 about misleading information, including claims about savings, tax credits, or program incentives. These are complaints, not proof that every named company violated a rule. They do show why homeowners should slow down and compare the paperwork.

Use this checklist before hiring a residential solar installer in Chicago.

## 1. Is the company authorized for the program it is selling?

If the salesperson is offering an Illinois Shines project, verify the company through the program's current Approved Vendor and Designee resources. Illinois Shines says installing companies must be certified Distributed Generation Installers and in good standing with the Illinois Commerce Commission. A project submitted to Illinois Shines must also be installed by a qualified person under the program's rules.

Ask:

- What is the legal name of the Approved Vendor?
- Which company is the installer or Designee actually performing the work?
- Can you show me the current program listing and ICC certification?
- Who is responsible if the sales company and installation company are different?
- Have I checked the program's public complaint and violations reports for both entities?

Do not rely on a salesperson's claim that the company is “state approved.” Verify the exact legal entities named in the proposal and contract.

## 2. Can I review the Illinois Shines Disclosure Form before signing?

For projects submitted to Illinois Shines, the customer must receive and review a completed Disclosure Form before signing the installation contract or subscription agreement. The form is designed to make key offer terms easier to compare.

Ask for the completed form early enough to read it without a salesperson standing over you. Compare it with the contract and the verbal pitch. Check the system size, expected production, price, financing or subscription terms, incentive details, and any fees or escalators. If the form and contract do not match what you were told, ask for corrected documents before signing.

Save the Disclosure Form, proposal, contract, and all versions of the savings estimate. Do not let a salesperson complete an electronic signature process for you or rush you through documents you have not read.

## 3. What is the actual total price, not just the monthly payment?

Ask for three comparable proposals if possible: cash purchase, solar loan, and lease or power-purchase agreement (PPA), where available. Each has different ownership, tax, maintenance, and transfer implications.

For a loan, ask for:

- Cash price before financing.
- Loan principal, APR, term, monthly payment, and total scheduled payments.
- Any dealer fee or financing charge included in the quoted system price.
- Whether the payment changes over time and under what conditions.
- Whether a balloon payment or prepayment penalty applies.

For a lease or PPA, ask who owns the equipment, how the payment escalates, what happens if you sell the home, and what the buyout or transfer process costs. Ask for the complete payment schedule, not only the advertised first-year payment.

Be cautious with promises that a tax credit or incentive will automatically cover a loan balance. Tax rules and eligibility can change, and a homeowner's tax situation is personal. Verify current federal and state tax treatment with an independent tax professional. Do not treat an assumed tax refund as guaranteed cash.

## 4. How was the production and savings estimate calculated?

Ask for the proposed system design and the assumptions behind its annual production estimate. Review roof orientation, tilt, shading, trees, nearby buildings, panel count, inverter type, and expected system losses.

Ask the installer to show:

- Estimated annual kilowatt-hour production.
- The utility rate and escalation assumptions used in the savings model.
- How much electricity you are expected to use at the time it is produced.
- What happens to excess electricity under the applicable utility tariff.
- Whether the model includes degradation, downtime, and maintenance assumptions.

A savings estimate is not a guarantee. Compare the proposal against your actual electric bills, not a generic household profile. Ask for the model in a format you can review after the sales meeting.

## 5. Is your roof ready for solar?

A solar system can last for decades. Installing it on a roof near the end of its useful life can create an expensive removal-and-reinstallation project.

Ask for an honest roof assessment before signing. How old is the roof? Are there signs of leaks, damaged decking, or worn flashing? If replacement is likely in the next several years, price that work separately and decide whether it should happen before solar installation.

Clarify whether roof repairs are included, who performs them, and whether the roofing contractor is a separate subcontractor. Get the roofing warranty and solar roof-penetration warranty in writing. Ask who pays if a leak appears around a mounting point and how quickly the company responds.

## 6. Who handles Chicago permits and utility approval?

Chicago's Department of Buildings offers an Express Permit pathway for certain small-scale residential solar photovoltaic projects. The city's published eligibility rules include system and building conditions; for example, small-scale systems with inverter output of 13.44 kW or less may qualify when the specified design requirements are met. Not every roof or system qualifies, and larger or more complex projects may require a different permit process.

Ask the installer which Chicago permit path applies to your project and request the permit application or issued permit number. Confirm who is responsible for drawings, structural calculations, electrical work, inspections, and corrections. Also ask who submits the interconnection application to your electric utility and who confirms permission to operate before the system is switched on.

Do not assume that panels being physically installed means the system is approved to operate. Keep copies of the permit, inspection sign-offs, utility approval, and final system documents.

## 7. What warranties and service obligations are included?

Separate the warranties. Solar proposals may include panel product and performance warranties, inverter warranties, racking warranties, workmanship coverage, and roof-penetration coverage. They are not necessarily provided by the same company.

Ask who handles a service call if the installer closes, changes ownership, or stops answering. Get response-time commitments, monitoring access, warranty claim procedures, and any service-trip charges in writing. Confirm whether monitoring equipment, internet connectivity, and replacement labor are included.

## Before you book

- Verify the Approved Vendor, installer/Designee, and required credentials.
- Review the Illinois Shines Disclosure Form before the contract.
- Compare total cost and total payments, not only the monthly amount.
- Have an independent person review financing and tax assumptions.
- Check roof condition before panels are installed.
- Confirm Chicago permits, inspections, and utility interconnection responsibilities.
- Get separate equipment, workmanship, and roof warranties in writing.

Solar can be a good long-term investment for the right home and contract. But a low monthly payment is not the same thing as a low total cost. Make the company show its math before you sign.
