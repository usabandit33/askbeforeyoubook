If your San Diego roof needs replacement and solar panels are already mounted on it, you may need a specialist to remove the array, protect and store the equipment, coordinate with the roofer, and reinstall and test the system. This is often called solar detach-and-reset. It is not simply a roofing add-on. The work can affect roof penetrations, electrical connections, equipment warranties, utility interconnection records, and the workmanship warranties of both the roofer and solar contractor.

The local market has a substantial installed base. The City of San Diego's 2025 Climate Action Plan annual-report appendix, released June 9, 2026, estimates residential behind-the-meter solar generation at 1,006 in 2025, up from 450 in 2019. The report's figure is generation, not a count of systems or homes, but it documents a growing residential solar base. The California Public Utilities Commission's Solar Consumer Protection Guide specifically tells homeowners to ask what it will cost to remove and reinstall panels if a roof needs replacement later, and what steps the provider will take to prevent roof leaks.

## 1. Verify the solar contractor's license classification

- **Ask:** *"Which California contractor license classification covers the detach-and-reset work, and who will be the contractor of record?"*
- **Request:** The company's legal name, CSLB license number, classification, current status, and proof of insurance.
- **Check:** Verify the license directly through the Contractors State License Board (CSLB), not only through a salesperson's business card.

California recognizes the C-46 Solar Contractor classification for installing, modifying, maintaining, and repairing photovoltaic systems. The City of San Diego's current Information Bulletin 301 says that reinstallation of existing roof-mounted PV removed for roof-covering renewal must be performed by a qualified contractor holding a C-10 Electrical or C-46 Solar license. Other work on the roof may require a separate properly licensed roofing contractor. Ask which company is responsible for each scope and do not assume one license automatically covers every trade involved.

If the roofer says its own crew will disconnect and reinstall the solar array, ask for the exact license classification and written explanation of how it meets the city's requirements. Verify any subcontractor too.

## 2. Confirm the permit and inspection plan before panels come off

- **Ask:** *"Who will obtain the electrical permit for reinstalling the existing array, and will you provide the permit number and inspection sign-off?"*
- **Request:** The original approved PV plan set, existing permit history, proposed permit documents, and the schedule for any pre-removal inspection.

San Diego's Information Bulletin 301, updated May 2026, addresses existing roof-mounted PV systems removed to renew the roof covering. It says the original approved record plan set must accompany the submitted PV plans to confirm the system was permitted and installed. A pre-removal inspection may be required. The reinstallation permit does not authorize changes to the original equipment or system.

That means you should locate the original plans before signing. If the plans are missing, the system was modified over time, or the array includes newer equipment, ask the City of San Diego Development Services Department how the work should be documented. Do not let the contractor assume that an old permit covers a changed system.

The City also says an electrical permit is required to reinstall existing roof-mounted PV removed for roof renewal. Get the permit responsibility in the contract and confirm that the contractor will schedule and pass the required inspection. If your property is in unincorporated San Diego County rather than the City of San Diego, county rules may apply instead.

## 3. Make the removal and storage scope specific

- **Ask:** *"How will you document, label, protect, transport, and store each panel, inverter, optimizer, microinverter, rail, and attachment?"*
- **Request:** Before-removal photographs, equipment inventory, serial numbers, condition report, and a written storage and handling plan.

A complete detach-and-reset quote should state how the system will be shut down, electrically isolated, disconnected, removed, labeled, and protected. Ask whether the price includes the array, rails, flashing, roof attachments, conduit, wire management, and any equipment that must be replaced because it cannot be reused.

Clarify where the equipment will be stored, who is responsible for loss or damage, whether it will be protected from weather, and how long storage is included. If the roofer's schedule changes, ask whether additional trips or extended storage create extra charges. Get those rates in writing before the work begins.

## 4. Coordinate the roofer and solar installer in one written schedule

- **Ask:** *"Who is responsible for the roof being ready before the array is reinstalled, and how will both contractors confirm the roof work is complete?"*

The roofer and solar contractor have separate scopes that meet at the roof penetrations. Ask the roofer to specify the roof covering, underlayment, flashing, and workmanship warranty. Ask the solar contractor to explain how the original attachments will be reinstalled or replaced, how penetrations will be sealed, and whether the new roof material requires a different attachment method.

Do not assume that the solar company's roof-leak promise replaces the roofer's warranty, or vice versa. Ask each contractor to state in writing which company is responsible if a leak appears around a solar attachment after the system is back in place. If the roof warranty has conditions about third-party penetrations or mounting hardware, obtain the manufacturer's or roofer's written approval before installation.

## 5. Confirm whether the system will be altered

- **Ask:** *"Is this a true like-for-like reinstall, or are you proposing to change panel layout, inverter, capacity, wiring, or equipment?"*
- **Request:** A written list of all equipment that will be reused, replaced, or changed, with model and serial numbers.

The City of San Diego says the reinstallation permit for a system removed for roof renewal does not allow alteration of the original equipment or system. If a contractor proposes a different inverter, new panels, a different layout, or an added battery, ask the city and your utility what additional plans, permits, inspections, or interconnection steps may be required before approving the change.

Also ask whether a change could affect your existing utility rate arrangement or interconnection agreement. Do not rely on a verbal promise that a modification will preserve your current billing treatment. Get the utility's answer for your specific system and proposed scope.

## 6. Get a complete price and clear change-order terms

- **Ask:** *"Does this price include shutdown, removal, storage, reinstallation, replacement hardware, permits, inspections, system startup, and monitoring verification?"*

Compare at least two written bids using the same equipment inventory and roof scope. Ask each bidder to list the number of panels, attachment points, rails, inverters, and any special access equipment. Identify excluded items such as damaged modules, brittle wiring, failed connectors, unavailable mounting hardware, or repairs to an existing code issue.

Require written approval before additional work. If the contractor discovers damaged components or an unsafe pre-existing condition, ask for photographs, the reason the work cannot proceed, and a separate price. Avoid a quote that promises one flat price but gives the contractor unlimited discretion to add charges.

## 7. Require commissioning records and a final walkthrough

- **Ask:** *"What tests will you perform after reinstallation, and what documents will I receive before final payment?"*

The contractor should verify that the system is operating after reinstallation and provide a clear record of the work. Ask for photos of the completed attachments and flashing interfaces, permit and inspection records, equipment serial numbers, inverter or monitoring status, and a commissioning or startup report appropriate to the system.

Compare post-reinstallation monitoring with the system's prior operation, allowing for weather and seasonal differences. Confirm that monitoring access works and that no inverter faults or disconnected devices remain. Keep the original system plans, removal inventory, roof invoice, solar invoice, permit records, warranties, and final test documentation together.

## Warning signs

- The contractor cannot provide a current C-10 or C-46 license for the PV reinstallation scope.
- The company says no permit is needed without confirming the exact property jurisdiction and work.
- The original approved PV plans are missing and no one has asked the city how to proceed.
- The quote does not identify who protects the panels or pays for equipment damaged in storage.
- The roofer and solar installer each disclaim responsibility for leaks at the mounting penetrations.
- The contractor plans to change equipment under a permit intended only for reinstallation.
- The company will not provide post-installation test results, monitoring verification, or inspection records.

## Before you book

- Find the original PV plans, permits, equipment list, and utility interconnection records.
- Verify the solar contractor's CSLB license and the roofer's separate license.
- Confirm San Diego permit and pre-removal inspection requirements for your address.
- Compare itemized removal, storage, reinstallation, and replacement-hardware costs.
- Coordinate roof completion, flashing, attachment details, and warranty responsibility in writing.
- Require written approval for any change to the original array.
- Obtain inspection records, equipment inventory, and post-installation operating documentation.

This checklist focuses on the City of San Diego. Nearby cities and unincorporated San Diego County may have different permit processes. Confirm the authority having jurisdiction for your parcel, and verify current CSLB, city, county, utility, and manufacturer requirements before work begins.
