Boston homeowners often hire painters, window contractors, carpenters, or deleading specialists for work in older housing without realizing that the legal category of the job may change once painted surfaces are disturbed. Massachusetts says paid renovation, repair, or painting in pre-1978 housing that disturbs more than the applicable threshold must be performed by a licensed Lead-Safe Renovation contractor. Deleading is a different, more formal service with its own licensing and clearance expectations. The cheapest quote is not a bargain if the contractor cannot legally perform the work or cannot document that the home was left safe.

## 1. Identify the job before comparing prices

- **Ask:** “Is this ordinary repair, lead-safe renovation, or deleading?”
- **Request:** A written scope describing the rooms, surfaces, square footage, dust-control plan, and whether the contractor is promising a Lead Law compliance result.
- **Check:** Whether the company is licensed for the exact category it is selling.

A contractor who casually says “we will just encapsulate it” may be describing a real method, but that phrase is not a substitute for a written hazard assessment. Ask who tested the paint, what hazards were found, and whether the proposed work is intended to produce a compliance letter, a clearance report, or simply a cosmetic repair.

## 2. Verify Massachusetts credentials

Massachusetts requires licensed lead-safe renovation contractors when covered work disturbs painted surfaces in pre-1978 homes. Deleading work requires a licensed deleader, and a licensed supervisor must be on site while the work is in progress.

- **Ask:** “What is your Massachusetts license number, and who will be the on-site supervisor?”
- **Request:** A current license record, proof of general liability insurance, workers’ compensation coverage, and references for similar older Boston homes.
- **Verify:** Search the state’s licensing and contractor resources yourself. A logo on a truck is not evidence.

If the property is a rental or a child under age six lives there, ask whether Massachusetts Lead Law compliance obligations apply. Do not assume an owner-occupied home is exempt from every lead-safety rule. The requirement depends on the property, the occupants, and the work being done.

## 3. Make containment and cleanup part of the contract

The contract should say how the contractor will isolate the work area, protect occupants, control dust, handle waste, clean daily, and document the final condition.

- Ask whether occupants must leave and for how long.
- Ask how HVAC openings, floors, porches, and neighboring units will be protected.
- Ask who pays for testing, clearance, re-cleaning, and return visits if the first clearance fails.
- Require change orders in writing before extra work begins.

A vague promise to “clean up when we are done” is not enough for lead-related work. The scope should identify the person responsible for final cleaning and the test or inspection that closes the job.

## Warning signs

- The contractor says licensing is unnecessary because the work is “just painting.”
- The quote has no dust-control, containment, or waste-disposal language.
- The company wants a large cash deposit before giving a written contract.
- The contractor will not identify the on-site supervisor.
- The price changes after testing, but the contract does not explain how.
- The contractor promises a compliance letter without describing who will perform the required inspection or clearance.

## Before you book

- Confirm the home’s age, occupancy, and whether it is a rental.
- Get the proposed work classified in writing.
- Verify the exact Massachusetts license category and on-site supervisor.
- Request insurance certificates and at least two comparable references.
- Compare written scopes, not just totals.
- Put containment, cleanup, testing, clearance, warranty, and payment milestones in the contract.
- Confirm local Boston permit or notification requirements before work starts.

Boston’s rules and Massachusetts lead requirements can change by project type. Verify the current requirements for the exact address and scope before signing.
