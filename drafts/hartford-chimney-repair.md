A chimney problem is not something to ignore. Creosote buildup, damaged masonry, a cracked flue liner, or a blocked vent can create real fire and carbon-monoxide risks. But an urgent warning from a stranger on your doorstep is not the same thing as a verified diagnosis.

Connecticut's Department of Consumer Protection (DCP) issued a chimney-service consumer alert in November 2025 after receiving 48 complaints involving chimney-related services since the previous fall. The agency described reports of door-to-door sellers claiming an urgent structural problem existed, offering a low initial price, and later charging several times the estimate. The point is not that every chimney contractor is suspect. It is that homeowners should separate inspection evidence from a high-pressure sales pitch.

Use these questions before hiring a chimney repair company in Hartford or the surrounding towns.

## 1. Is this a cleaning, an inspection, or an actual repair?

Those are different services, and Connecticut draws an important line between them.

A chimney sweep can clean and inspect the chimney and identify defects. Connecticut DCP says chimney sweeps do not need a home-improvement registration just to sweep, but people performing chimney repairs or installations must hold an active Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration. Repairs can include chimney-cap work, liner repair or replacement, and mortar replacement.

Ask:

- Are you quoting a cleaning, an inspection, or a repair?
- What specific defect did you find?
- Can you show me photographs of the affected area and explain where it is?
- Who will perform the repair, and what is that person's Connecticut HIC registration number?

**Red flag:** A salesperson says a cleaning crew can immediately perform structural repairs without explaining the required registration or showing you the problem.

## 2. Can you document the defect before quoting the fix?

A statement such as “your chimney is unsafe” is not a diagnosis. Ask for a written inspection report with photographs, the location of the defect, and the recommended repair.

For a proposed liner replacement, ask what material is specified, why the existing liner is unsuitable, how the new liner will be sized for the appliance, and whether the work includes required connections and termination details. For masonry work, ask whether the quote covers only repointing, a partial rebuild, a crown repair, flashing, or a full chimney rebuild. Those are not interchangeable scopes.

If the contractor recommends a major rebuild, get a second opinion from another qualified professional before authorizing it, unless an immediate hazard requires you to stop using the fireplace or appliance. If you suspect a safety issue, do not use the fireplace until it has been evaluated.

## 3. Verify the contractor's registration and insurance

Before you sign, ask to see the contractor's Connecticut-issued HIC registration certificate and identification. Verify the registration through the state's eLicense lookup rather than relying on a number printed on a flyer.

Ask for a certificate of general liability insurance and confirm that the policy is active with the insurer. Ask whether the company uses subcontractors and, if so, who is responsible for their work and insurance.

A chimney sweep's voluntary membership or certification can be useful evidence of training, but it is not a substitute for the state registration required for repair work. Ask what training or credentials the actual person inspecting or repairing your chimney holds.

## 4. Does the written contract describe the complete job?

Connecticut requires home-improvement work to be covered by a written contract. Before signing, check that it identifies the contractor and registration number, describes the work and materials, states the price and payment schedule, and includes the required notice of your right to cancel.

Ask the contractor to itemize:

- Labor, materials, disposal, equipment, and any access or roof charges.
- The exact liner, cap, crown, flashing, or masonry work included.
- What is excluded and what conditions could trigger a change order.
- The start date, expected completion date, and cleanup plan.
- Separate warranties for materials and workmanship.

Do not sign a blank or partly completed agreement. Do not rely on a verbal promise that a “free inspection” is not binding. Keep a copy of the estimate, contract, photos, and messages.

## 5. Who handles permits and inspections?

DCP advises homeowners to contact their local building official when a chimney repair may require a building permit. Requirements can depend on the work and the municipality. Ask the contractor to identify the permit needed, who will apply for it, and whether the price includes permit fees. Then verify the answer with the building department for your actual town. Hartford city requirements may differ from those in West Hartford, East Hartford, or another nearby municipality.

Do not let a contractor tell you that a permit is unnecessary simply because the job is urgent. If a permit is required, get the permit number and final inspection documentation.

## 6. Is the payment schedule tied to completed work?

Avoid paying the entire price before materials arrive or work begins. Connecticut DCP recommends dividing payments into stages that correspond to project progress. Ask what deposit is needed for materials, what milestone triggers the next payment, and what must be finished before the final payment is due.

The state says qualifying home-improvement contracts include a three-business-day cancellation period. Saturday counts as a business day in Connecticut. Do not sign a waiver of that right just because a salesperson says it is standard. If you believe there is an emergency, independently confirm the hazard and understand the consequences before waiving a protection.

## Before you book

- Get a written diagnosis with photographs, not just an alarming verbal claim.
- Verify the HIC registration for the person or business doing repairs.
- Compare at least two itemized repair proposals for major work.
- Confirm permit responsibility with your local building official.
- Read the contract, cancellation notice, warranty, and payment milestones before signing.
- Decline pressure to pay immediately by cash, wire transfer, or prepaid card.

A safe chimney matters. So does knowing exactly what you are buying. If the contractor cannot show you the defect, explain the repair, and document the agreement, pause before handing over a deposit.
