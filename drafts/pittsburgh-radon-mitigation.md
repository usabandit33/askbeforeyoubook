Radon is an invisible, odorless radioactive gas that can enter a home from the soil. You cannot judge the risk by smell, appearance, neighborhood reputation, or the age of the house. Testing is the only way to know whether a particular home has an elevated reading.

Pennsylvania is a particularly important market for this service. The Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) says approximately 40% of homes tested in the state have radon levels above the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's action guideline of 4 picocuries per liter (pCi/L). That is a statewide result, not a Pittsburgh-only estimate, and it does not mean every home in Allegheny County has a radon problem. DEP also reports typical permanent mitigation costs of about $800 to $1,200, while actual bids depend on the home's foundation, layout, access, and system design. The agency recommends testing even when a mitigation system is already installed.

If a Pittsburgh-area test result is high, do not let urgency replace due diligence. A good contractor should be able to explain the proposed system, show current Pennsylvania credentials, put the scope and price in writing, and tell you how the result will be checked after installation.

## 1. Verify Pennsylvania radon certification before comparing prices

Pennsylvania requires DEP certification for people and firms performing radon testing and mitigation, subject to limited statutory exemptions. Testing and mitigation are separate activity types. A company name on a truck or a general contractor's license is not a substitute for the appropriate radon credential.

- **Ask:** *"What is your current Pennsylvania DEP radon mitigation certification number, and which certified individual will be responsible for this installation?"*
- **Ask:** *"Is the business itself listed as a certified firm, or are you relying on an individual's certification?"*
- **Check:** Search the DEP Radon Services Directory for the person and company. The directory is updated monthly; the version available when this checklist was reviewed listed updates through October 1, 2026.
- **Request:** A copy or clear photo of the current DEP identification card or certificate, plus the written proposal showing the responsible certified person.

Do not assume a national association membership, training certificate, or manufacturer's badge satisfies Pennsylvania's certification requirement. If the directory and the contractor's paperwork do not match, pause and ask DEP to clarify before paying a deposit.

## 2. Ask what the system will actually do in your house

Many homes use a sub-slab depressurization system, but the right approach depends on the building. A contractor should inspect the foundation and explain the route for suction piping, the fan location, the exhaust termination, electrical needs, and any obstacles such as a finished basement, crawlspace, multiple slabs, or additions.

- **Ask:** *"Where will you create suction, where will the pipe run, and where will the exhaust terminate?"*
- **Ask:** *"What parts of the foundation or basement are not covered by this design, and why?"*
- **Ask:** *"Will the fan be outside, in an attic, or in another location? How will noise, weather exposure, and service access be handled?"*
- **Ask:** *"What happens if the first system does not reduce the radon level enough?"*

A useful quote should describe the proposed layout and the assumptions behind it. Be cautious if a salesperson promises a guaranteed result without inspecting the home, cannot explain where the exhaust will go, or gives the same design and price to every property.

## 3. Compare written scopes, not just the bottom line

Ask at least two qualified contractors to quote the same general scope. A lower price may omit electrical work, difficult drilling, a second suction point, patching, permits where required, or post-installation testing. A higher quote is not automatically better either.

- **Ask:** *"Is this a complete installed price, including the fan, piping, electrical connection, sealing, mounting, and cleanup?"*
- **Ask:** *"What conditions could increase the price, and will you get my written approval before doing extra work?"*
- **Ask:** *"Are permits or inspections required for this specific installation by the City of Pittsburgh or the municipality where my home is located?"*
- **Request:** A line-item scope, payment schedule, expected installation date, warranty terms, fan model and warranty information, and a written change-order process.

Do not pay the entire amount upfront. Tie payments to clearly described milestones and keep the final payment until the agreed work and documentation are complete.

## 4. Make post-installation testing part of the agreement

A running fan does not prove that the home's radon level has been reduced adequately. Ask how and when the system will be tested after installation, who will perform the test, and whether that tester holds the appropriate Pennsylvania certification.

- **Ask:** *"When should the post-mitigation test be performed, and who is responsible for arranging it?"*
- **Ask:** *"Will I receive the test report showing the measurement period, device or method, test location, and final result?"*
- **Ask:** *"What is the process if the result remains at or above 4.0 pCi/L?"*
- **Ask:** *"What maintenance should I perform, how will I know if the fan stops, and how quickly can you service it?"*

DEP recommends testing every two years, including in homes with mitigation systems. Keep the original test, the installation proposal, system diagram, warranty, fan information, and every later test result with your home records.

## Warning signs before you book

- The contractor refuses to provide a current DEP certification number.
- The company pressures you to sign immediately because of a home sale or inspection deadline.
- The proposal has no system description, no total price, or no process for approving additional work.
- The contractor promises a specific radon reading without explaining how it will be verified.
- The company will not explain fan monitoring, maintenance, warranty coverage, or what happens if the post-installation test is still high.
- You are told that a mitigation system means you never need to test again.

## Before you book

- Confirm that the test result belongs to your home and that the test was performed correctly.
- Verify the contractor and responsible individual in the current Pennsylvania DEP Radon Services Directory.
- Compare written system designs and complete installed prices.
- Confirm permits and electrical requirements with your municipality.
- Get warranty, maintenance, post-installation testing, and follow-up responsibilities in writing.
- Keep copies of all test results and installation records.

Pittsburgh is only the local example here. Allegheny County includes many municipalities, and building, electrical, and permit requirements can differ. Confirm current requirements with the municipality that has jurisdiction over your property and use DEP's current directory rather than relying on an old screenshot or a contractor's verbal assurance. If you are unsure how to interpret a test result, contact the Pennsylvania DEP Radon Division or the EPA before deciding what work is necessary.
