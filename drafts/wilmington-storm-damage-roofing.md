After a windstorm or hail event, a roofing salesperson may appear before you have even had time to inspect the yard. A prompt roof inspection can be useful. Signing a contract on the spot is a different matter.

Delaware's Department of Justice has described a current consumer-protection investigation involving alleged storm-chasing sales practices. In October 2026, the Attorney General announced an administrative complaint alleging that a home-improvement company misled homeowners into believing they were signing a form for a free inspection when the form was actually a contract with a large cancellation penalty. The allegations are not a final finding of liability, but they are a timely reminder to read every page before signing.

This checklist is for homeowners in Wilmington and New Castle County comparing roof inspections, storm repairs, or full replacement proposals.

## 1. Are you being asked to sign for an inspection or for the job?

Ask the salesperson to write down exactly what the document authorizes. Does it permit a roof inspection only? Does it authorize emergency tarping? Does it appoint the company to negotiate with your insurer? Does it commit you to use the contractor if insurance pays? Those are materially different agreements.

Before signing anything:

- Read every page, including attachments and fine print.
- Ask whether the document is binding and what happens if you do not hire the company.
- Look for cancellation charges, liquidated damages, assignment-of-benefits language, or a promise to pay a percentage of an insurance settlement.
- Take a copy home and review it without the salesperson waiting at your door.

**Red flag:** “This is just permission for a free inspection” while the document contains a price, cancellation penalty, or obligation to hire the company.

## 2. Can the contractor show you the storm damage?

Ask for dated photographs of each damaged area and a written explanation of what the contractor believes happened. Have them distinguish storm damage from age, wear, installation defects, and pre-existing conditions.

Ask:

- Which slopes, shingles, flashing, vents, gutters, or roof penetrations are damaged?
- What evidence supports hail or wind damage rather than ordinary wear?
- Is a repair technically possible, or is replacement being recommended? Why?
- Can you provide photographs before and after temporary protection?
- Will you provide a line-item estimate rather than a single total?

Do not let a contractor promise that your insurance company will approve a claim. The insurer decides coverage under your policy. A roofer can document observed conditions and prepare an estimate, but should not misrepresent damage or encourage you to file a claim for work that is not covered.

## 3. Verify Delaware contractor registration

Delaware requires businesses performing construction services, including roofing, to register with the Department of Labor's Office of Contractor Registration before work begins. Ask for the contractor's current registration certificate and verify the business through the state's contractor registry.

Also ask for:

- The exact legal business name and physical mailing address.
- General liability insurance information and a current certificate.
- Proof of workers' compensation coverage where required.
- The name of the person supervising the crew.
- Whether any subcontractors will be used and who is responsible for them.

A salesperson's branded shirt or truck is not proof that the company is registered or insured. Verify the business you will actually pay, not just the name of a lead-generation website.

## 4. Does the contract satisfy Delaware's home-improvement rules?

Delaware's Home Improvement Services Regulations require a written contract that meaningfully describes the work and materials, states the contract price and other material terms, and provides an approximate completion date unless the contract clearly explains that no date is being provided. The contractor must fill in relevant final terms before asking you to sign.

The contract should make clear:

- Whether this is a repair, partial replacement, or full roof replacement.
- The roof area and materials, including shingle manufacturer, product line, color, underlayment, flashing, ventilation, and drip edge.
- Removal and disposal of existing materials.
- Decking replacement pricing and how hidden damage will be documented and approved.
- Permit fees, cleanup, magnetic nail sweep, and protection of landscaping.
- Start and estimated completion dates.
- Workmanship warranty, manufacturer warranty, and who handles warranty claims.

Delaware's rules also prohibit misleading a homeowner about whether a document is an estimate or a binding contract. The state provides a summary of consumer rights for home-improvement transactions. Ask for it before signing.

## 5. Who pulls the permit and schedules inspections?

Do not assume the permit is included just because the contractor says the project is “code compliant.” Ask whether the work requires a permit in Wilmington or the specific municipality where your home sits, who will obtain it, and who will arrange inspections.

For Wilmington, check with the City of Wilmington's Licenses and Inspections department. If your property is in an unincorporated part of New Castle County or another municipality, the relevant building office may be different. Ask for the permit number and keep the final inspection record with your home documents.

## 6. How does the estimate relate to your insurance claim?

If you are making an insurance claim, keep the contractor's estimate separate from the insurer's scope of loss. Compare them line by line. Ask the roofer to identify differences in quantities, materials, code items, and labor assumptions rather than simply saying the insurer is “low.”

Before agreeing to any financing or payment arrangement, ask:

- What is the total contract price if insurance pays less than expected?
- Am I responsible for the deductible and any non-covered upgrades?
- Are supplements submitted only for documented work actually required?
- Does the contract require payment before the insurer issues funds?
- Are there financing charges, cancellation fees, or late fees?

Never agree to waive your deductible or falsify an invoice. If the claim is disputed, speak with your insurer or a qualified insurance professional rather than relying on the contractor's promise.

## 7. What happens if the roof leaks before the project starts?

Ask whether temporary tarping or emergency repairs are a separate service, what they cost, and whether that amount is credited toward the final job. Get photos of the temporary work and a written receipt. Temporary protection should not silently turn into a full replacement commitment.

## Before you book

- Do not sign a “free inspection” form until you know whether it is a contract.
- Verify the company's Delaware contractor registration and insurance.
- Get photographs and a written explanation of the damage.
- Compare at least two itemized estimates.
- Confirm permits with the correct local building office.
- Read cancellation language, warranties, payment terms, and insurance-related clauses.
- Keep copies of every estimate, text, photograph, contract, and payment.

A roof is expensive enough without adding a contract dispute. A legitimate contractor should be comfortable giving you time to read the paperwork and compare the scope.
