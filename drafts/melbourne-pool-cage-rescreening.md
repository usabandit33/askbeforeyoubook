Melbourne’s coastal climate subjects aluminum pool enclosures and lanai screens to wind, salt air, and intense UV. Mesh becomes brittle; spline fails; individual panels blow out in storms. Many handymen avoid large multi-panel jobs, and some full-scale aluminum builders prefer new cages over repair work. Homeowners with shredded screens need a clear scope: one panel, several bays, or a full rescreen—and whether the frame itself is still sound.

Before you book, get the damage mapped and priced in writing so you are not surprised by frame repairs mid-job.

## 1. Is this a single-panel repair or a full rescreen?

- **Ask:** *"Based on the age and condition of the mesh, is replacing individual panels enough, or will a full rescreen cost less per panel and last longer?"*
- **Red flag:** Pushing a full cage replacement when only a few panels and the spline are worn.

If mesh is only a few years old and damage is localized, panel replacement is usually the economical choice. If screens are UV-brittle or sagging throughout, a full rescreen often costs less per panel and resets the enclosure for another decade. Ask the company to walk the cage and show you which bays are repairable.

## 2. What is the condition of the aluminum frame and hardware?

- **Ask:** *"Will you inspect beams, fasteners, cables, door closers, and anchors before quoting, and will frame repairs be priced separately from screen work?"*
- **Red flag:** A screen-only quote that ignores bent beams, corroded fasteners, or a door that will not latch.

Rescreening a compromised frame wastes money. Coastal corrosion and storm loads can loosen hardware or bend members. A solid estimate lists screen work and any structural or hardware items as separate lines so you can prioritize.

## 3. Which mesh and spline will you install?

- **Ask:** *"What mesh type are you quoting—standard, no-see-um, or high-visibility—and will the spline and fasteners be replaced with the panels?"*
- **Red flag:** Vague “standard screen” with no product detail or no mention of spline replacement.

Mesh choice affects insects, visibility, and durability. If you are already paying for a rescreen, upgrading mesh on high-traffic or waterfront exposures can be inexpensive relative to a second visit. Confirm that worn spline is replaced so new mesh is held correctly.

## 4. How do you handle storm damage and insurance documentation?

- **Ask:** *"If this is storm-related, will you document existing damage with photos and an itemized scope suitable for an insurance claim?"*
- **Red flag:** Starting work before photos or refusing to separate storm damage from deferred maintenance.

After high wind, screens are often designed to release so the frame survives. Photograph damage before anything is moved. Ask whether the company has experience with insurance estimates and whether permits are required for your scope through the City of Melbourne or Brevard County building department.

## 5. Timeline, access, and workmanship warranty

- **Ask:** *"When can you start, how long will the cage be open, and what workmanship warranty covers the new mesh and spline?"*
- **Red flag:** An open-ended schedule with no weather contingency or no written warranty on labor.

Pets, children, and pool use matter while panels are down. Get a realistic start window and a clear completion expectation. Licensed, insured crews that pull required permits and stand behind the work reduce the chance of a second failure in the next storm season.

## Before you book

Map single-panel versus full rescreen, inspect the frame and hardware, specify mesh type, document storm damage when relevant, and lock in schedule and warranty in writing. Melbourne and Brevard enclosures take a beating; a precise scope is cheaper than a vague one.
