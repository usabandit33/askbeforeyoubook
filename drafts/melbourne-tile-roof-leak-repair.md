Many Melbourne and Brevard County homes carry Spanish clay or concrete tile roofs that handle Florida heat well but depend on intact underlayment and metal flashing to stay waterproof. Coastal salt air, intense sun, summer downpours, and wind can degrade valley flashing, step flashing around chimneys and vents, and the underlayment beneath the tiles long before the tiles themselves fail. Water then travels through the attic and appears as interior stains, often far from the actual entry point.

Large roofing companies frequently push full replacements. Targeted leak detection and flashing or underlayment repair is a different scope, and it requires a Florida-licensed roofing contractor who is willing and equipped to work carefully on tile without causing additional breakage.

## 1. Separate a localized repair from a full reroof decision

A single slipped or broken tile, a corroded valley, or a failed section of underlayment does not automatically mean the entire roof must be replaced. Conversely, widespread underlayment failure, repeated leaks in multiple areas, or tiles that are no longer available can make a full reroof the more rational long-term choice.

**Ask:** "Based on what you find, is this a localized flashing or underlayment repair, or are you recommending a full reroof—and why?"

Request a written explanation of the observed conditions, the proposed scope, and the expected remaining service life of the surrounding roof. Do not accept a one-sentence "needs a new roof" conclusion without supporting detail.

## 2. Confirm a Florida roofing contractor license

Roofing work in Florida, including tile roof repair and reroofing, requires a licensed roofing contractor. A general contractor cannot simply perform tile roofing work under a general license in most situations; the specialty is regulated. Brevard County and the City of Melbourne issue permits for repair or replacement of roof covering, including tile.

**Ask:** "What is the exact legal business name and Florida Certified or Registered Roofing Contractor license number that will appear on the permit and my contract?"

Verify the license through the Florida DBPR license search. Confirm that the company carries workers' compensation and liability insurance. Ask who will be on the roof and how the crew protects surrounding tiles from breakage.

## 3. Require a proper diagnosis, not a guess from the ground

Effective tile-roof leak detection often involves inspecting the attic for the path of water, examining valleys and penetrations, lifting or removing selected tiles to inspect underlayment, and testing suspected areas. Walking on tile without proper distribution of weight cracks tiles and creates new leaks.

**Ask:** "How will you locate the actual entry point, and will you protect the surrounding tiles while working?"

Ask whether the contractor uses roof jacks, walk boards, or other methods that avoid point-loading fragile tiles. Request photos of the damaged underlayment or flashing before it is covered again.

## 4. Understand what a complete localized repair includes

A thorough repair scope for a tile roof leak typically addresses:

- Removal of the affected tiles without unnecessary breakage.
- Inspection and replacement of damaged underlayment in the affected area.
- Repair or replacement of corroded valley, step, or pipe flashing with materials suitable for coastal exposure.
- Proper fastening and sealing consistent with Florida product approvals and manufacturer instructions.
- Reinstallation of tiles (matching as closely as practical) and cleanup of debris.
- Attic inspection for secondary damage and drying recommendations if needed.

**Ask:** "Does your price include underlayment, flashing, matching tiles, and cleanup, or are those extras?"

Clarify whether the contractor will source matching tiles or whether you must supply them. Coastal salt air accelerates corrosion of ordinary galvanized metal; ask what flashing metal is being used.

## 5. Confirm permits and the 25-percent / reroof rules that may apply

Brevard County requires a residential reroof permit for repair or replacement of roof covering, including tile. The City of Melbourne has its own building department for properties inside city limits. Florida's building code and local practice also address situations in which the extent of repair triggers broader reroof or underlayment requirements (sometimes discussed in connection with the "25% rule" and related provisions). The exact threshold and documentation depend on the jurisdiction and the current code edition.

**Ask:** "Will you pull the required roofing permit for this address, and does the scope trigger any additional underlayment or reroof requirements?"

Do not assume a small repair needs no permit. Keep the permit and final inspection documentation.

## 6. Compare written proposals and payment terms

Useful proposals should state the diagnosed cause, the exact work to be performed, materials (underlayment type, flashing metal, tile source), permit responsibility, warranty on workmanship and materials, and a clear price. Avoid large deposits before the diagnosis is complete and the scope is written.

**Ask:** "What warranty applies to the repaired area, and what is excluded if new leaks appear elsewhere on the roof?"

Get everything in writing. Photograph the completed work and retain the invoice and permit records for insurance and future sale of the home.

## Warning signs

- The contractor is not a licensed Florida roofing contractor.
- The company refuses to get on the roof or inspect the attic and instead quotes a full replacement from the ground.
- The estimate has no breakdown of underlayment, flashing, or tile work.
- The crew walks on tiles without protection and dismisses breakage as inevitable.
- The contractor says no permit is needed for a repair that involves underlayment or flashing replacement.
- A large upfront payment is demanded before any diagnosis.
- Matching tiles or coastal-grade flashing are treated as optional extras without discussion.

## Before you book

Obtain a written diagnosis that distinguishes localized repair from full reroof, verify the Florida roofing contractor license and insurance, require a scope that includes underlayment and appropriate flashing, confirm the permit plan for your specific jurisdiction (City of Melbourne or Brevard County), and keep the documentation. Verify current permit and code requirements with the applicable building department before work begins. This is general consumer guidance only and is not engineering or code-compliance advice for your specific roof.
