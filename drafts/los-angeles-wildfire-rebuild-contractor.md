Rebuilding after a wildfire is not an ordinary remodeling project. Homeowners may be working with an insurer, an architect, a lender, a debris-removal team, a permit center, and several subcontractors while also trying to make decisions under grief and time pressure. Los Angeles County has published special guidance because wildfire survivors are vulnerable to unlicensed contractors, rushed agreements, and confusing rebuilding claims.

A contractor who says “we can get you started immediately” may be offering a real solution, a half-truth, or a problem with a hard hat. Verify the path before you sign.

## 1. Confirm the contractor is properly licensed for the work

California generally requires the appropriate contractor license for home construction and reconstruction. The Los Angeles County Department of Consumer and Business Affairs and the Contractors State License Board both direct consumers to verify licenses and watch for unlicensed operators after disasters.

- **Ask:** What CSLB license classification covers this work, and who is the qualifying individual?
- **Request:** Current license number, business name exactly as registered, proof of general liability insurance, workers' compensation coverage, and a list of subcontractors.
- **Verify:** The license status, classification, workers' compensation information, and disciplinary history through CSLB before signing.

Do not accept a contractor's screenshot as the whole investigation. Search the license yourself and make sure the contract names the same legal entity you verified.

## 2. Separate the rebuild scope from the insurance estimate

- **Ask:** Is this contract for debris clearing, structural reconstruction, design-build services, temporary protection, or all of the above?
- **Request:** A line-item scope, allowances, exclusions, unit prices, change-order rules, and a schedule tied to permit and inspection milestones.
- **Ask:** Who communicates with the insurer, and who owns disagreements about code upgrades or concealed damage?

Insurance estimates often change as demolition, engineering, and code requirements become clearer. A responsible contract explains how changes are priced and approved. Be wary of anyone promising that insurance will “cover everything” before the insurer and the jurisdiction have accepted the relevant scope.

## 3. Understand permit pathways and self-certification claims

Los Angeles has established One-Stop Permit Centers and local self-certification programs to speed certain wildfire rebuild projects. That does not mean permits, inspections, building codes, or a certificate of occupancy disappear.

- **Ask:** Which city or county department has jurisdiction over my address?
- **Request:** Permit application numbers, the plan-check path, the name and license of any self-certified professional, and the inspection schedule.
- **Ask:** If the project uses a self-certification or expedited process, what documents still must be submitted and who is legally responsible for compliance?

The City of Los Angeles describes a Self-Certification Program for licensed professionals and a residential single-family wildfire rebuild path. The California state guidance also warns that builders using special federal disaster-loan options must still meet building-code, health, safety, inspection, and certificate-of-occupancy requirements. A shortcut in review is not a shortcut around accountability.

## 4. Demand a contract that protects the homeowner

California disaster-rebuilding guidance emphasizes written contracts and careful payment decisions. Ask for:

- A complete written contract with start and completion terms.
- The payment schedule, deposit amount, retainage if any, and rules for stored materials.
- A process for approving change orders in writing before extra work begins.
- Proof that subcontractors are paid and that lien-release documentation will be provided.
- The warranty period, exclusions, and the person responsible for warranty calls.

Avoid pressure to sign immediately because “prices go up tomorrow.” A legitimate emergency stabilization job can be separated from the full rebuild contract so you have time to compare the larger commitment.

## 5. Use local recovery resources as a second set of eyes

Los Angeles County has published workshops and a contractor-hiring guide for wildfire survivors. Use those resources before accepting a referral from a stranger, a social-media message, or a person who appears at your property without an appointment.

- **Ask:** Can the contractor provide references for completed wildfire rebuilds in the same jurisdiction?
- **Request:** Photos, permit closeout records, and the names of references who will discuss delays, change orders, and final payment.
- **Check:** Whether your city or county offers a recovery center, permit navigator, or public workshop.

If a contractor asks you to pay them to “unlock” a permit, FEMA benefit, SBA option, or insurance payment, stop and verify the claim with the agency directly.

## Warning signs

- The license number is missing, expired, or belongs to a different legal entity.
- The contractor demands a large deposit or cash-only payment.
- The proposal is a lump sum with no scope, allowances, or change-order process.
- The contractor promises insurance approval, permit approval, or a guaranteed completion date.
- The contractor says self-certification means inspections are unnecessary.
- The contractor refuses to identify subcontractors or provide lien-release documentation.
- The contractor pushes you to sign before you have reviewed the contract with your insurer, attorney, or trusted adviser.

## Before you book

- [ ] Verify the contractor and qualifying individual through CSLB.
- [ ] Confirm the city, county, and permit center with jurisdiction over the property.
- [ ] Compare at least two detailed scopes when the situation allows.
- [ ] Separate emergency stabilization from full reconstruction.
- [ ] Put payment milestones, change orders, warranties, and lien releases in writing.
- [ ] Save all insurance estimates, permit records, photos, and contractor communications.
- [ ] Confirm what inspections and certificate-of-occupancy steps remain before final payment.

Wildfire recovery requires compassion, but compassion does not require abandoning documentation. Keep your eyes open, ask direct questions, and insist that every shortcut still leave a clear paper trail.
